# Outcome Frameworks

Bloom's Taxonomy verb banks, the ABCD outcome format, audience-portrait templates, and transformation-statement scoring. Load when writing learning outcomes, audience profiles, or transformation statements.

---

## 1. Bloom's Taxonomy verb banks

Bloom's Taxonomy describes six cognitive levels, from lower-order recall to higher-order creation. Outcomes should be written at the level the course genuinely reaches. A course that promises to "teach" a skill but only covers recall is underselling or overpromising.

### Level 1: Remember (recall facts, recognize information)

Use when: the student needs to know something before they can do anything with it.

Verbs: define, list, name, recall, recognize, identify, state, label, match, repeat, reproduce, select, arrange, memorize.

Example outcome: "List the five stages of the customer journey in order."

### Level 2: Understand (explain in own words, interpret meaning)

Use when: the student needs to grasp a concept, not just recite it.

Verbs: explain, describe, summarize, paraphrase, classify, compare, interpret, distinguish, discuss, restate, translate, give examples of, infer.

Example outcome: "Explain why a project timeline changes when a dependency is delayed."

### Level 3: Apply (use knowledge in a new situation)

Use when: the student will perform a procedure or use a concept in practice.

Verbs: apply, use, execute, carry out, implement, demonstrate, calculate, solve, practice, produce, operate, complete, show, construct.

Example outcome: "Apply the 80/20 rule to a provided client task list and identify the two highest-impact actions."

### Level 4: Analyze (break into parts, examine relationships)

Use when: the student needs to break something apart and understand how the pieces relate.

Verbs: analyze, differentiate, distinguish, compare, contrast, examine, break down, deconstruct, organize, attribute, outline, diagram, categorize, question, test.

Example outcome: "Analyze a sample landing page and identify the three elements most likely to reduce conversion."

### Level 5: Evaluate (judge, assess, justify a position)

Use when: the student needs to make a judgment or defend a decision.

Verbs: evaluate, assess, judge, critique, justify, defend, prioritize, rank, recommend, select, argue, rate, appraise, decide.

Example outcome: "Evaluate two competing pricing strategies for a service business and justify a recommendation based on margin and positioning."

### Level 6: Create (produce something new, design, build)

Use when: the student leaves the course with a created artifact or designed system.

Verbs: create, design, build, produce, develop, construct, formulate, compose, plan, propose, invent, generate, write, draft, assemble.

Example outcome: "Create a complete 90-day content calendar for a new social media account, including content types, publishing frequency, and performance benchmarks."

## 2. The ABCD outcome format

ABCD provides a structure that ensures outcomes are complete and assessable.

- **A (Audience):** Who is the learner? Usually implied in course outcomes; made explicit in module-level outcomes when the sub-audience differs.
- **B (Behavior):** The observable action the learner performs (the Bloom's verb + the object).
- **C (Condition):** The context or constraints under which the learner performs the behavior. ("Given a provided data set," "using only free tools," "without referring to notes.")
- **D (Degree):** The standard of performance. ("With at least 80% accuracy," "in under 15 minutes," "to a level acceptable for client presentation.")

Not every outcome needs all four elements. A clean B+C is often enough for course-level outcomes. B+C+D is ideal for module assessments.

**Example with all four:**
"Given a raw client interview transcript (C), the student will extract and categorize (B) at least five distinct user insights (D) using an affinity mapping method."

**Example with B+C only (common for course outcomes):**
"Using the project template provided in Module 3, create a complete brand identity brief ready to share with a designer."

## 3. Verbs to avoid (and why)

These verbs appear measurable but are not. They describe internal states that cannot be observed or assessed.

| Banned verb | Why it fails | Replace with |
|-------------|--------------|--------------|
| Understand | No observable behavior; impossible to assess | Explain, apply, demonstrate |
| Know | Same problem as understand | Define, identify, list, use |
| Appreciate | Describes an attitude, not a skill | Evaluate, articulate the value of |
| Be aware of | Vague; sets no performance bar | Recognize, distinguish, identify |
| Learn about | Describes the activity, not the result | Apply, demonstrate, produce |
| Grasp | Informal; no observable behavior | Explain, summarize, use |
| Get familiar with | No clear performance bar | Identify, navigate, use |

## 4. Audience-portrait templates

### Template 1: The career changer

Use when: the student is moving from one field or role into a new one.

- Situation: Working full-time in [CURRENT FIELD]. Feels stuck or misaligned with their current path. Has been researching [TARGET FIELD] for [TIMEFRAME].
- Pain: Lacks the credentials, portfolio, or network that [TARGET FIELD] professionals seem to have. Has tried applying and been rejected, or is afraid to apply at all.
- Prior attempts: Watched YouTube tutorials, read articles, perhaps tried a generic course. Knowledge is scattered; confidence is low.
- Goal: Get [specific entry-level milestone: first client, first job, first project] in [TARGET FIELD] within [TIMEFRAME].
- Vocabulary: "breaking in," "no experience," "how do I even start," "is it too late," "I'm not sure I'm qualified."

### Template 2: The practicing professional who wants to level up

Use when: the student already works in the field but wants to move to the next tier.

- Situation: Working as a [ROLE] for [TIMEFRAME]. Competent but not advancing. Feels like they are missing a skill or system that more senior peers have.
- Pain: Keeps hitting the same ceiling: clients who do not see their value, projects that go over budget, opportunities that go to more visible peers.
- Prior attempts: Learned by doing, watched some training, maybe took a conference workshop. Nothing has systematically closed the gap.
- Goal: [Specific professional milestone: higher-fee clients, a leadership role, a published body of work].
- Vocabulary: "next level," "charge more," "be taken seriously," "get better clients," "build a system."

### Template 3: The complete beginner

Use when: the student is starting from zero with no prior exposure to the topic.

- Situation: Recently decided to pursue [TOPIC] for personal, creative, or entrepreneurial reasons. No formal background. May feel embarrassed by the extent of their ignorance.
- Pain: Overwhelmed by the volume of beginner content that assumes prior knowledge. Tried free resources that go too fast or too deep too soon.
- Prior attempts: YouTube rabbit holes, a free crash course, asking friends. Gets started but stalls when things get technical.
- Goal: A specific, achievable first project or first result that proves to themselves they can do this.
- Vocabulary: "I have no idea where to start," "beginner friendly," "step by step," "can you explain it simply," "I don't want to waste time learning the wrong thing."

### Template 4: The time-pressed practitioner

Use when: the student is competent but needs a faster, more efficient way to get results.

- Situation: Experienced in [TOPIC] but spending too much time on tasks that should be faster. Has a growing workload with no room to slow down and improve.
- Pain: Their current process is slow, inconsistent, or dependent on tools they have outgrown. They know there is a better way but have not had time to find it.
- Prior attempts: Asked peers for advice, read blog posts, tried a few new tools. Nothing stuck because there was no structured path.
- Goal: A specific efficiency gain: cut time on [TASK] by [AMOUNT], systematize [PROCESS], or stop doing [THING] manually.
- Vocabulary: "faster," "streamline," "I keep doing this by hand," "there has to be a better way," "I need a system."

### Template 5: The side-hustle builder

Use when: the student is trying to monetize a skill or interest alongside their current work.

- Situation: Has a skill, hobby, or expertise that others have told them they should monetize. Works a day job. Limited time, limited startup budget.
- Pain: Does not know how to find clients, price services, or structure the side business without it taking over their personal time.
- Prior attempts: Set up a social media account, maybe tried Upwork or Fiverr, got a few low-paying gigs. Stalled because the approach was not systematic.
- Goal: A sustainable side income of [AMOUNT] per month from [SKILL/SERVICE] within [TIMEFRAME], without quitting their day job.
- Vocabulary: "side hustle," "extra income," "I don't know how to get clients," "I don't want to be salesy," "how do I price this."

## 5. Transformation-statement scoring rubric

Rate the transformation statement on three dimensions, 1-3 each.

### Dimension 1: Specificity of the before state (1-3)

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 | The before state names a specific situation, constraint, or pain that only this audience would recognize. |
| 2 | The before state is clear but general; it could apply to many audiences. |
| 1 | The before state is vague ("struggling," "not sure where to start") with no distinguishing detail. |

### Dimension 2: Concreteness of the after state (1-3)

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 | The after state is a specific, observable change in behavior, output, or situation. It can be verified. |
| 2 | The after state is positive but abstract ("more confident," "better equipped"). |
| 1 | The after state is a feeling or aspiration, not a concrete change. |

### Dimension 3: Clarity of the mechanism (1-3)

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 | The "by [mechanism]" clause names what the course specifically does: a method, a system, a framework, a deliverable. |
| 2 | The mechanism is implied but not stated. |
| 1 | No mechanism is named; the statement is a before-after without any "how." |

**Total: 9 = strong, ready to use. 6-8 = revise at least one dimension. Below 6 = rewrite.**

### Scored examples

**Example A:** "Freelance designers will go from charging hourly rates that undervalue their work to packaging and selling project-based offers that consistently earn $3,000 or more, by applying the productized-service framework in Module 2."
Scores: Specificity 3 (specific audience and pain), Concreteness 3 (specific measurable outcome), Mechanism 3 (named framework and module). Total: 9.

**Example B:** "Students will go from feeling overwhelmed about their finances to feeling confident and in control."
Scores: Specificity 1 (vague), Concreteness 1 (feeling, not behavior), Mechanism 1 (none). Total: 3. Rewrite required.

**Example C:** "Marketing professionals will go from manually reporting metrics every week to generating accurate performance reports in under 30 minutes using the reporting templates provided."
Scores: Specificity 2 (clear audience and pain, but audience is broad), Concreteness 3 (specific time saving), Mechanism 3 (named deliverable). Total: 8. Revise specificity by narrowing the audience description.
