# Audience and Outcome Definer: Worked Example

Sample brand (for illustration only): Theo Ramirez is a commercial real estate agent with 11 years of experience who also teaches a quarterly workshop on lease negotiation for first-time business owners. He wants to turn that workshop into a self-paced online course.

---

## Inputs

- Course topic: Lease negotiation for first-time business owners renting commercial space.
- Intended student: Small business owners who are signing their first commercial lease and feel out of their depth negotiating with a landlord or commercial real estate broker.
- Primary transformation: Go from intimidated and uninformed to able to negotiate key lease terms confidently without hiring a lawyer for every conversation.

---

## Output

### 1. Audience Portrait

You are a small business owner who just found a space you are excited about. You have spent months looking, and the landlord has sent over a lease. It is 40 pages long, full of terms you do not recognize, and the landlord's broker is friendly but clearly motivated to get you to sign quickly.

You have never signed a commercial lease before. You run a [TYPE OF BUSINESS] and know your trade well, but this feels like a different game entirely. You are worried about locking yourself into something you will regret. You have heard horror stories: a friend who got stuck in a 10-year lease with no exit clause, someone who found out their "all-in" rent was actually triple-net with surprise expenses.

You have googled some of the terms. The results were either too technical (written for attorneys) or too generic (not specific to your situation). You asked your accountant; they told you to talk to a lawyer. You called a lawyer; the retainer was $3,000 before they would read the lease.

What you actually want is simple: to walk into that negotiation knowing what is worth fighting for, what is standard, and what to say so you do not look like an amateur.

**Pain:** Feeling out of your depth in a high-stakes, one-sided conversation with professionals who do this every day.
**Prior attempts:** Googling lease terms (too technical), asking a general attorney (too expensive for a first negotiation), relying on the landlord's broker to "explain" terms (conflict of interest).
**Goal:** Leave the negotiation with fair terms on rent, lease length, build-out allowance, and renewal options, without paying $3,000 in legal fees for a standard lease review.
**Vocabulary:** "What does this actually mean?", "is this standard?", "can I negotiate this?", "what should I push back on?", "I don't want to get taken advantage of."

---

### 2. This Course Is Not for You If...

- You are an experienced operator renewing your third or fourth commercial lease. You already know the landscape; this course covers foundations, not advanced strategy.
- You are a commercial real estate agent or property manager. The course is designed to teach tenants how to negotiate, not professionals.
- You are signing a residential lease. Commercial and residential lease law are different; this course covers commercial only.
- You are looking for jurisdiction-specific legal advice. This course teaches negotiation strategy, not legal guidance. Complex or high-value leases benefit from an attorney review even after taking this course.
- You want a done-for-you service. This course teaches you to negotiate yourself. It does not provide lease review, representation, or a drafted addendum.

---

### 3. Primary Transformation Statement

First-time commercial tenants will go from feeling intimidated and underprepared when receiving a lease to confidently negotiating the five highest-impact terms before signing, using a plain-language review process and a reusable negotiation framework.

Transformation statement score: Specificity 3 (specific audience and pain), Concreteness 3 (five terms, specific process and framework), Mechanism 3 (named deliverables: review process and framework). Total: 9.

### 4. Learning Outcomes by Module

| Module | Learning Outcomes |
|--------|------------------|
| Module 1: How Commercial Leases Work | 1. Define the five most common commercial lease structures (gross, net, triple-net, modified gross, percentage). 2. Distinguish which lease structure is being used by reading the "rent" and "expenses" clauses. 3. Identify the three clauses that most commonly disadvantage first-time tenants. |
| Module 2: Rent and Escalation | 1. Calculate effective monthly rent under a triple-net arrangement given a provided expense schedule. 2. Evaluate a rent escalation clause and determine whether the rate is standard for the local market. 3. Propose three alternative escalation structures with supporting rationale. |
| Module 3: Lease Length, Options, and Exit Clauses | 1. Compare the risk profiles of 3-year, 5-year, and 10-year lease terms for early-stage businesses. 2. Identify whether a renewal option is tenant-favorable or landlord-favorable based on notice period and rate terms. 3. Draft a plain-language request for a co-tenancy clause or early-termination right using the provided template. |
| Module 4: Build-Out, Allowances, and Improvements | 1. Explain the difference between a tenant improvement allowance and a turnkey build-out. 2. Calculate the net cost of a build-out given a provided allowance and estimated construction quote. 3. Identify which improvement improvements revert to the landlord at lease end under a standard "surrender" clause. |
| Module 5: Negotiating with Confidence | 1. Apply the BATNA framework to a provided lease scenario to determine negotiation priorities. 2. Use the five-step counterproposal structure to respond to a landlord's initial terms in writing. 3. Evaluate a completed negotiation scenario and identify which terms were successfully improved and which were not worth contesting. |

---

### 5. Terminal Outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

- Read a standard commercial lease and identify the clauses that matter most for a first-time tenant, without needing a legal dictionary.
- Calculate your true all-in monthly cost under any lease structure, including triple-net scenarios.
- Negotiate at least three key lease terms in writing using the counterproposal framework, before signing anything.
- Decide confidently when a term is worth contesting and when accepting it is the right move.
- Use the Lease Review Checklist to evaluate any future commercial lease in under two hours.
