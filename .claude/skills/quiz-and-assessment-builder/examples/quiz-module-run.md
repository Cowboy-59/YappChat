# Worked Example: Quiz and Assessment Builder

Sample brand (for illustration only): Priya Sandhu is a productivity coach who teaches a self-paced course called "Deep Work by Design" to knowledge workers who want to reclaim focus in open-plan offices and remote setups. Her audience is mid-career professionals, mostly aged 30-45. The course is $397, hosted on Teachable.

---

## Inputs provided

- **Module:** Module 3, "Designing Your Focus Environment"
- **Learning objective:** By the end of this module, students will be able to identify at least three environmental factors that reduce their personal focus and redesign their workspace to minimize them.
- **Key concepts:** sensory friction, attention residue, environmental cues, proactive vs. reactive workspace setup
- **Format:** Mix of multiple-choice and one short-answer scenario
- **Number of questions:** 7 (formative check: 3 questions mid-module, end-of-module quiz: 4 questions)

---

## Output

### Cognitive level

The verb "identify and redesign" maps to **Level 3: Apply**. Students must not just recall definitions; they must take what they learned and apply it to their own workspace. The end-of-module questions will emphasize scenario-based application. The mid-module formative check will establish foundational recall.

---

### Formative Check: Module 3 (Mid-Module, Unscored)

**Question 1 (Remember)**
What term describes the mental carry-over from a previous task that reduces your ability to fully focus on a new one?

A. Context switching  
B. Attention residue  
C. Cognitive overload  
D. Decision fatigue

**Correct:** B

**Rationale:** Attention residue (coined by researcher Sophie Leroy) refers specifically to the phenomenon where part of your attention stays on a prior task even after you have moved on. Context switching describes the act of switching, not the lingering mental cost. Cognitive overload and decision fatigue are related but distinct concepts.

---

**Question 2 (Understand)**
A student keeps email open on a second monitor "just in case" while doing deep work. Which concept from this module best explains why this reduces focus quality?

A. The inbox is a source of sensory friction.  
B. Environmental cues prime the brain for reactive behavior.  
C. The student lacks a proactive workspace setup.  
D. Both B and C apply here.

**Correct:** B (or D, if the course has introduced both concepts by the mid-module point)

**Rationale:** A visible inbox is an environmental cue that signals "check messages," which primes the student for reactive, interruption-driven behavior rather than deep work. Sensory friction (A) typically refers to physical or auditory distractions, not visual cues. A proactive setup (C) is the solution, not the diagnosis.

---

**Question 3 (True/False)**
True or false: The most effective way to improve focus is to develop stronger willpower to ignore distractions.

**Correct:** False

**Rationale:** This module argues that environmental design beats willpower as a focus strategy. Willpower is finite and depletes throughout the day. Redesigning the environment removes the need to resist distraction in the first place, which is more durable and requires less ongoing effort.

### End-of-Module Quiz: Module 3 (Scored, Pass threshold: 75%)

**Total points: 20 | Pass: 15/20**

---

**Question 4 (Apply, 6 points)**
Marcus works from a home office. He notices he spends the first 30 minutes of every morning answering messages before he gets to his main project. He has tried setting an intention each night but it does not stick. Which two environmental changes would most directly reduce his reactive morning behavior?

A. Wake up 30 minutes earlier to get ahead of messages  
B. Turn off phone and email notifications and keep devices in another room until a set focus block begins  
C. Set a daily reminder alarm that says "do deep work first"  
D. Place his project notes and materials on his desk the night before as a visual cue for the morning
E. Subscribe to a time-management newsletter

**Correct: B and D** (3 points each)

**Rationale:** B directly removes the environmental cue (notification pings and visible devices) that triggers reactive behavior. D creates a positive environmental cue that primes deep work as the first action. A addresses timing but not the cue structure; C is a willpower-based solution (the reminder does not redesign the environment); E is not an environmental change.

---

**Question 5 (Apply, 5 points)**
Describe one sensory friction source in your own current workspace, and explain the specific change you would make to reduce it. Use the terminology from this module.

*Open-ended. Rubric: 3 points for correctly identifying a sensory friction source using module terminology; 2 points for describing a concrete, implementable environmental change (not a willpower or habit-based change).*

---

**Question 6 (Analyze, 5 points)**
Two colleagues both struggle with afternoon focus. Colleague A rearranges her desk to face a wall and uses noise-canceling headphones. Colleague B schedules a daily 2pm "focus hour" in her calendar and tells her team not to disturb her during that time. Compare their approaches. Which relies more on environmental design, and which relies more on social/behavioral agreements? Which approach does this module favor for long-term consistency, and why?

*Strong answer: Colleague A's approach is more purely environmental (physical workspace change, sensory friction reduction). Colleague B's approach is behavioral/social (scheduling, communication). The module favors environmental design for long-term consistency because it removes reliance on others' cooperation and does not deplete personal willpower. Award full marks for correctly categorizing both approaches and linking the preference to the module's core argument.*

---

**Question 7 (Apply, 4 points)**
Which of the following workspace changes would be considered proactive design (preventing distraction before it occurs) vs. reactive management (responding to distraction after it appears)?

| Change | Proactive or Reactive? |
|---|---|
| Putting your phone on silent when you notice you are distracted | |
| Leaving your phone in another room before your focus block starts | |
| Closing your email tab after you have already spent 20 minutes on it | |
| Setting your status to "Focusing" in your team messaging app before starting work | |

**Correct answers:**
- Phone on silent when distracted: Reactive
- Phone in another room before the block: Proactive
- Closing email tab after 20 minutes: Reactive
- Setting status before starting: Proactive

**Rationale:** Proactive design acts before the distraction can occur, changing the environment in advance. Reactive management responds after the disruption has already begun, which means attention residue has likely already accumulated.
