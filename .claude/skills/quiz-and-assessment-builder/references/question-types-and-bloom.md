# Question Types and Bloom's Taxonomy

## The six levels and what they demand

Bloom's Taxonomy describes six cognitive levels. The verb in a learning objective tells you which level the module targets. Match your question type to that level; questions that are too easy (testing recall when you want application) create false confidence.

| Level | Typical verbs in objectives | What the student must do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Remember | define, recall, list, identify, name | Retrieve stored information |
| 2. Understand | explain, describe, summarize, classify | Interpret and restate meaning |
| 3. Apply | use, solve, demonstrate, calculate, execute | Use knowledge in a new situation |
| 4. Analyze | compare, differentiate, break down, examine | Pull apart structure or relationships |
| 5. Evaluate | judge, justify, critique, recommend | Make a reasoned judgment |
| 6. Create | design, build, compose, formulate, construct | Assemble something new |

---

## Level 1: Remember

**Best question types:** multiple-choice definitions, true/false fact statements, fill-in-the-blank with a word bank.

**Example 1 (multiple-choice):**
Which term describes the practice of spacing review sessions over time to improve long-term retention?

A. Interleaving  
B. Spaced repetition  
C. Elaborative interrogation  
D. Chunking

*Correct: B. Spaced repetition.*

**Example 2 (true/false):**
True or false: The brain processes visual information faster than text-only information on average.

*Correct: True.*

**Example 3 (fill-in-the-blank):**
The three stages of a standard sales call are _______, _______, and _______.

*Correct answers drawn from the module's specific terminology.*

---

## Level 2: Understand

**Best question types:** multiple-choice where the student explains in their own words, matching, short-answer summaries.

**Example 1 (multiple-choice):**
A student learns a formula but then explains it to a friend using a completely different example. Which term best describes what the student did?

A. Memorization  
B. Transfer  
C. Rehearsal  
D. Retrieval practice

*Correct: B. Transfer. Distractor note: "Rehearsal" is tempting because the student repeated the formula, but rehearsal is repetition of the same material, not rephrasing with a new example.*

**Example 2 (short answer):**
In two sentences, describe the difference between a lead magnet and a tripwire offer.

*Rubric: Must mention that a lead magnet is free and exchanges for contact information, and that a tripwire is a low-cost paid offer designed to convert a subscriber into a buyer.*

**Example 3 (matching):**
Match each email type to its primary purpose. [Types: welcome, re-engagement, broadcast. Purposes: reactivate inactive subscribers, introduce new subscribers to the brand, share a timely message to the whole list.]

---

## Level 3: Apply

**Best question types:** scenario-based multiple-choice, "what would you do next" questions, short-answer problem solving.

**Example 1 (scenario multiple-choice):**
A course creator has 847 email subscribers and wants to launch a live cohort. They have 14 days until cart open. Which move would most directly increase enrollment?

A. Redesign the sales page  
B. Send a three-email pre-launch warm-up sequence  
C. Lower the course price by 20%  
D. Add a new module to the curriculum

*Correct: B. A pre-launch warm-up sequence builds intent and surfaces objections before cart open, which directly improves conversion. Redesigning the page is useful but takes longer to show results; discounting reduces revenue without addressing intent; adding a module does not affect enrollment.*

**Example 2 (scenario short answer):**
Your module teaches a five-step framework. A student emails you saying they completed step 3 but got stuck. Write the two-sentence reply you would send using the framework itself as the coaching tool.

**Example 3 (applied calculation):**
If a course creator needs $5,000 in revenue and the course costs $497, how many students must enroll? Show your work.

*Correct: 11 students (rounded up from 10.06). Award full marks for showing the division even if the student rounds differently, provided their rounding logic is stated.*

## Level 4: Analyze

**Best question types:** compare-and-contrast questions, case study analysis, identifying errors in a worked example.

**Example 1 (case analysis):**
Read the two sales email subject lines below. Identify which one is more likely to convert a warm subscriber and explain the specific reason using at least one principle from this module.

Subject A: "Big news from us!"  
Subject B: "Your cart closes in 6 hours (here's what's inside)"

*Strong answer: Subject B creates urgency with a time element, names a specific benefit ("here's what's inside"), and respects the subscriber's time. Subject A is vague and gives no reason to open. Award marks for citing the specific principle (curiosity gap, specificity, or urgency).*

**Example 2 (spot the error):**
Below is a learning objective a course creator wrote. Identify what is wrong with it and rewrite it correctly.

"By the end of Module 4, students will understand marketing."

*Issues: no measurable verb, no specific scope, no stated context. Corrected example: "By the end of Module 4, students will be able to write a one-paragraph customer persona using the three-field framework taught in this module."*

**Example 3 (compare-and-contrast):**
Compare synchronous (live) and asynchronous (self-paced) course delivery on three dimensions: student completion rate, creator time investment, and scalability. Conclude with a recommendation for a solo creator selling to beginners.

## Level 5: Evaluate

**Best question types:** justify-your-answer scenarios, critique a sample, recommend and defend.

**Example 1 (justify):**
A creator is choosing between a $97 mini-course and a $997 flagship course. They have a list of 500 subscribers and no reviews yet. Which would you recommend, and why? Cite at least two factors from the module in your answer.

*Strong answer references trust-building (no social proof yet favors a lower-risk entry offer), time to revenue (mini-course ships faster), and the ability to use a mini-course purchase as a qualifier for the flagship upsell.*

**Example 2 (critique):**
Review the following module outline (provided). Evaluate whether it achieves the stated learning objective. Identify one strength and one area to improve.

**Example 3 (recommendation):**
A student has completed the course and wants to choose one platform to host their first course. Based on the criteria covered in Module 6, recommend one platform and defend your choice in three sentences.

---

## Level 6: Create

**Best question types:** open-ended project prompts, design-a-plan challenges, write/build/compose tasks.

**Example 1 (design):**
Using the module frameworks, design a three-email welcome sequence for a course about [topic]. Write the subject line and a one-paragraph body for each email.

**Example 2 (build):**
Create a one-page sales page outline for a course you would build in the next 90 days. Include the headline, the promise, three key benefits, and a call to action.

**Example 3 (compose):**
Write the learning objective for each of the three modules in your planned course. Each objective should use an active, measurable verb and name a specific skill or outcome.

## Writing effective distractors (wrong answer options)

The quality of a multiple-choice quiz depends almost entirely on the quality of the wrong answers.

**Principles:**
- Each distractor must be plausible to someone who almost understood the concept but got one thing wrong.
- Avoid "obviously wrong" options that are there only to fill space.
- Avoid "all of the above" and "none of the above"; they test test-taking skill, not content knowledge.
- One distractor should represent the most common misconception about the topic.
- One distractor should use correct vocabulary in the wrong context (this catches students who memorized terms without understanding them).
- Keep all options roughly the same length; if the correct answer is longer, students learn to pick the longest one.

**Common mistakes to avoid:**
- Writing the correct answer in more formal or complete language than the distractors.
- Using "always" and "never" in wrong answers (students learn to avoid absolutes).
- Making the question answerable by logic without knowing the content.
- Using double negatives in the stem ("which of the following is NOT an incorrect way to...").
