# Course Validator: Worked Example

Sample brand (for illustration only): Maya Chen is a UX designer with eight years of experience, mostly at mid-size SaaS companies. She has helped three colleagues land UX jobs and wants to build a course teaching career changers how to build a portfolio strong enough to get hired, without a design degree.

---

## Inputs

- Course idea: "A course for career changers who want to break into UX design, focused on building a portfolio that gets callbacks."
- Intended audience: Mid-career professionals in non-design fields (marketing, project management, customer support) who want to move into UX.
- Price point: $397 self-study, including templates and a private community.

---

## Output

### Restated Concept

Mid-career professionals with no design background will learn to build a UX portfolio that demonstrates real design thinking, so they can get interview callbacks from hiring managers without a design degree.

User confirmed: yes, that is the concept.

---

### Demand Audit

**Search intent (score: 4)**

The problem has clear search phrases:
- "UX portfolio for career changers"
- "how to get into UX design without a degree"
- "entry-level UX portfolio examples"
- "UX design career change tutorial"
- "how to get hired in UX with no experience"

These are typed as how-to questions, which signals genuine problem-awareness. The phrase "UX portfolio" has strong search volume. The career-changer angle narrows it, but that specificity is an asset, not a liability. Score: 4.

**Community evidence (score: 5)**

Career-change and UX-specific communities are highly active on this exact topic. Posts asking "how do I break into UX with no degree?" and "rate my portfolio" are extremely common in design and career communities. Frustrated mid-career professionals discussing failed applications appear regularly. Existing members celebrate wins: "I just landed my first UX role after eight months." This is strong, recurring, specific evidence. Score: 5.

**Willingness to pay (score: 4)**

Career changers invest heavily in upskilling because the outcome (a new career) has a clear, large ROI. Competing courses in the UX career-change space exist at $97-$1,200. The $397 price point is mid-range for this topic. One concern: free bootcamp prep content is abundant, and some free programs include portfolio projects. The course needs to clearly differentiate from free options. Score: 4.

Demand dimension score: **4.3**

---

### Competitive Landscape

**Free alternative:** YouTube channels and blog posts covering UX portfolio basics. Good at explaining what to include (case studies, the problem/process/outcome structure) but weak on feedback, portfolio review, and the specific strategy for career changers who have no professional design work to show yet.

**Low-price competitor (under $150):** A self-paced UX fundamentals course covering Figma and portfolio basics. Comprehensive in tool skills; does not address the career-changer psychology or the specific challenge of building projects when you have no client work. The community component is minimal.

**Premium competitor (over $800):** Cohort-based UX career accelerators running $800-$2,500. Strong on accountability and cohort culture; weak on accessibility (time commitment is 10-15 hours per week for 12 weeks; out of reach for working professionals with families).

**The gap:** Career changers need portfolio guidance that specifically addresses their constraint: no prior design work to show. They need a self-paced format (because they have full-time jobs) and a credible community (because the cohort alternatives are too expensive or too time-intensive). Price gap confirmed between $150 and $800. Gap type: specificity gap plus format gap.

---

### Promise Stress-Test

**Is the outcome specific enough to be believable?**
Yes. "Get interview callbacks" is measurable and specific. It does not promise a job (which is beyond the course's control) but promises a portfolio strong enough to trigger callbacks, which the creator can influence. Pass.

**Can a student achieve it within the course?**
A complete portfolio is achievable in 8-12 weeks of part-time work. The promise is scoped to the portfolio, not the full job search. Pass.

**Can the creator credibly teach it?**
Maya has eight years of UX experience and has helped three colleagues build portfolios that got them hired. She has direct experience with the outcome. She should document those three successes as case studies before launch. Pass with one note: the case studies are the proof engine. They need to be in the course or on the sales page.

**Does the price match the perceived value of the outcome?**
A UX role typically pays $60,000-$90,000 at entry level. The 10x test: $397 price requires $3,970 in perceived value. A career change to a higher-paid role easily clears that threshold. Pass.

---

### Creator-Fit Check

Direct experience: yes. Maya has done the thing (worked in UX) and has guided others through the transition.
Proof: Tier 2, pending documentation. Three success stories exist but are not yet written up as case studies. This is the single most important pre-launch task.
Sustained energy: Maya has been discussing this topic for years, which is a strong signal. She will not burn out on the subject matter.

One gap: Maya should record a short "about me" video or write a credibility bio that explicitly connects her experience to the career-changer outcome, not just her design career.

---

### Verdict Scorecard

| Lens | Score (1-5) | Note |
|------|-------------|------|
| Demand | 4 | Strong search intent and community evidence; buying behavior confirmed |
| Competition | 4 | Clear gap between free content and expensive cohorts; price-format gap exists |
| Promise | 4 | Specific, achievable, credible; 10x value passes comfortably |
| Fit | 4 | Direct experience, emerging proof, high energy; case studies needed before launch |

**Verdict: Go.** All four lenses score 4. Build this course. One watch point on proof (case studies must be documented before launch).

---

### Fix List

1. **Write up the three success stories as mini case studies** (before recording a single lesson). Name the person (with permission) or anonymize them. Show where they started, what they built, and the outcome. These belong on the sales page and in the course intro module.

2. **Write a clear differentiator statement** for the sales page that names the career-changer constraint explicitly: "This course is built specifically for professionals who have no design work to show yet. Every project is designed to demonstrate your thinking, not your years of experience."

3. **Test the community component.** Before building it out, confirm whether the audience prefers async discussion (forum style) or live touchpoints (monthly calls). A quick poll or pre-launch survey question will answer this.
