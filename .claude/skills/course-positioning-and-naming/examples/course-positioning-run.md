# Worked Example: Course Positioning and Naming

Sample brand (for illustration only): Dani Okafor is a ceramic artist and educator who teaches hand-building techniques to adult beginners. She wants to launch a self-paced online course for people who have always wanted to make pottery but live too far from a studio or cannot afford regular classes. Her course covers coil building, slab work, and basic surface decoration. Price point: $247. She has a warm audience of 3,200 Instagram followers who follow her for her earthy aesthetic and calm teaching style.

---

## Inputs provided

- **Core topic:** Hand-building pottery from scratch for absolute beginners, no wheel required
- **Student frustration before:** "I want to make pottery but I don't live near a studio, classes are $80-$120/month, and I feel too intimidated to try without proper guidance."
- **Result after completing:** Students have made three finished pieces (a coil pot, a slab dish, and a decorated tile) using tools they can buy for under $40, and they feel confident enough to keep practicing independently.
- **Names already considered:** "Clay at Home," "The Beginner's Pottery Course"
- **Competing courses to differentiate from:** Other beginner pottery courses that focus on wheel throwing, or that require a kiln

---

## Transformation Arc

**Before:** Someone who loves the idea of making pottery but has no studio access, feels intimidated by wheel-throwing, and assumes ceramics requires expensive equipment and years of classes to get started.

**After:** A confident hand-builder who has completed three real pieces with basic tools, understands the core techniques well enough to practice independently, and has a new creative practice they can maintain at home.

---

## Name Candidates

1. **[Result Name] Built by Hand: Your First Three Pottery Pieces** - Promises the tangible deliverable (three pieces) and signals the hand-building distinction.

2. **[Timeline Name] 30 Days to Your First Handmade Pot** - Simple, achievable timeline. Strong for someone who has been procrastinating.

3. **[Audience Name] Pottery for People Who Don't Have a Wheel** - Directly names the situation. Immediately differentiates from wheel-throwing courses.

4. **[Transformation Name] From Studio-Envy to Making It Yourself** - Targets the specific emotional state (watching others make pottery and wishing you could).

5. **[Objection Name] No Studio, No Problem: Beginner Hand-Building at Home** - Addresses the main barrier head-on. Good for discovery (people searching "home pottery without a studio").

6. **[Method Name] The Coil-and-Slab Method: Pottery for Beginners** - Names the two core techniques, which signals depth and specificity for people who have done research.

7. **[Specificity Name] Hand-Building Pottery for Total Beginners (No Wheel, No Kiln Required)** - Extremely specific; works well as a subtitle but may be too long as a primary title.

8. **[Clever Name] Dirt Under Your Nails: A Beginner's Guide to Hand-Building** - Evocative, on-brand for Dani's earthy aesthetic. Needs a subtitle to convey what the course is.

9. **[Insider Access Name] What Pottery Teachers Never Tell Beginners: Hand-Building from Scratch** - Creates curiosity; good for warm audience who trusts Dani as an insider.

10. **[Challenge Name] The 3-Piece Challenge: Make Real Pottery at Home in a Month** - Action-oriented, specific deliverable, tight timeline. Works well for a motivated beginner.

---

## Positioning Angle

**Vs. a competing course (wheel-throwing courses, standard beginner pottery):**
Most beginner pottery courses teach wheel throwing, which requires renting studio time, an expensive wheel, and a level of skill that takes months to reach. This course teaches hand-building, a technique just as old and expressive as wheel work, that requires no wheel, no kiln (air-dry and oven-bake clay options are included), and tools available at any craft store. Students make real, finished pieces in the first week.

**Vs. figuring it out alone (YouTube tutorials, free content):**
Free pottery tutorials on YouTube are made for people who already know the basics. They skip the "why do I do it this way" that beginners need, and there is no sequence from easy to hard. This course is the only structured sequence built specifically for someone starting from nothing, with every step explained and every common beginner mistake addressed before it happens.

**Vs. doing nothing:**
The most common version of doing nothing here is watching studio pottery videos on social media and feeling a mix of admiration and low-grade envy. The course costs less than two months of a local pottery class and results in three actual pieces the student made themselves.

---

## Final Recommendation

**Primary: Pottery for People Who Don't Have a Wheel**
This name does everything at once: it signals who the course is for, differentiates it from wheel-throwing courses (the dominant category), and addresses the core barrier in the title itself. It is specific enough to be memorable and honest enough to pre-qualify the right student.

**Backup: The 3-Piece Challenge**
If Dani wants to run the course as a cohort or live challenge first to validate it, this name creates natural momentum. The three-piece deliverable is concrete and achievable, and the challenge frame suits an engaged Instagram audience.
