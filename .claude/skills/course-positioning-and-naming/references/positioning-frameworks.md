# Positioning Frameworks for Courses

Positioning is the answer to one question the potential student asks without knowing they are asking it: "Why this course instead of everything else I could do?" There are four main positioning frameworks used in course marketing. Choose the one that matches the creator's actual situation; misapplied positioning sounds hollow.

---

## Framework 1: Problem-Solution

**What it is:** You name the specific problem the audience has and position the course as the direct solution. The course exists because the problem exists.

**When to use:** When the problem is widely recognized and causes visible pain. Strongest for business, finance, health, and productivity courses. The audience already knows they have the problem; they are looking for the right solution.

**Questions to answer:**
- What is the exact problem this audience experiences? (Be specific. Not "I don't have enough clients." Instead: "I get one or two leads a month from word-of-mouth and have no idea how to add a consistent second source.")
- What does the problem cost them? (Money, time, stress, opportunity, identity.)
- Why have other solutions failed? (Too complicated, not specific to their situation, too expensive, required tools or skills they do not have.)
- Why does this course solve it when others have not?

**Positioning statement formula:**
"[Course name] is for [audience] who are struggling with [specific problem]. Unlike [alternatives], it [specific thing the course does differently]."

**Worked example:**
"Bookkeeping for Etsy Sellers is for handmade business owners who dread doing their taxes because their income is irregular and their expenses are all mixed up with personal spending. Unlike general bookkeeping courses, it uses Etsy-specific categories and the free tools Etsy provides, so no paid accounting software is needed."

---

## Framework 2: Category Creation

**What it is:** Instead of competing in an existing category, you define a new one that only your course can occupy. The category name becomes the positioning.

**When to use:** When the creator has a genuinely novel approach that does not fit existing categories, or when the existing category is so crowded that standing out requires reframing the entire conversation. Requires confidence; if the category sounds invented just to avoid competition, it will not land.

**Questions to answer:**
- What is the old way of solving this problem, and what is wrong with it?
- What is the new way this course teaches, and what makes it fundamentally different?
- Can you name the new category in a phrase the audience will immediately understand and believe?

**Positioning statement formula:**
"There are a lot of [existing category] courses. [Course name] is not that. It is [new category name]: [one sentence description of what that means for the student]."

**Worked example:**
"There are plenty of social media marketing courses. The Quiet Launch is not that. It is an enrollment marketing course: a system for selling a course to people who already know and trust you, without spending a day on content creation or ad spend."

---

## Framework 3: Against the Alternative

**What it is:** You position the course directly against the most common alternative the audience considers, making the comparison explicit and favorable.

**When to use:** When the main competition is not another course but a behavior (DIY, YouTube tutorials, hiring someone, reading a book, doing nothing). This framework is honest and direct, and it respects the audience's intelligence by acknowledging the alternatives exist.

**The three alternatives most course buyers consider:**
1. Free content (YouTube, blogs, podcasts): "I can figure this out for free."
2. Hiring a professional: "I should just pay someone to do this for me."
3. Doing nothing: "I don't really need this right now."

**Questions to answer:**
- What is the audience's primary alternative to buying this course?
- Why does the course win that comparison? (Speed, cost, customization, accountability, quality of outcome?)
- What does the alternative cost them that they may not have calculated?

**Positioning statement formula:**
"You could [alternative]. Here is what that costs you: [specific cost]. [Course name] gives you [specific result] in [timeframe], so [what they avoid by using the course]."

**Worked example (against free content):**
"You could spend six months piecing together tax advice from finance YouTube channels. Here is what that costs you: mismatched advice from creators in different tax situations than yours, no one to verify your work, and the anxiety of never knowing if you got it right. Freelance Finance 101 gives you the exact four-step process in two hours, built specifically for sole proprietors with irregular income."

**Worked example (against doing nothing):**
"Waiting until April to figure out your quarterly taxes costs most freelancers $600 to $2,000 in penalties and a very bad week. This course takes two hours and prevents both."

---

## Framework 4: Identity Transformation

**What it is:** You position the course not as a skill-builder but as a transformation of who the student is or how they see themselves. The course is about becoming someone, not just learning something.

**When to use:** When the target audience's real block is not information but identity (they don't believe they are the kind of person who can do this thing). Most effective for creative courses, entrepreneurship courses, and personal development topics. Less effective for technical courses where the student just needs the how.

**Questions to answer:**
- Who does the student believe they are right now that is holding them back? ("I'm not a business person," "I'm not creative," "I'm not a natural salesperson.")
- Who do they become after the course?
- What is one concrete thing they do in module 1 that begins to shift the identity?

**Positioning statement formula:**
"[Course name] is not just about [skill]. It is about becoming the kind of [identity label] who [specific behavior or capability]."

**Worked example:**
"Write Your First Novel is not just about finishing a manuscript. It is about becoming the kind of writer who shows up every day, finishes what they start, and trusts their own voice. By the end of week one, you will have written more than you wrote in the past year."

---

## Applying positioning to the course promise

Once the positioning framework is chosen, the course promise follows directly from it.

**Course promise format:** "After [course name], you will [specific outcome] even if [the most common objection or prior barrier]."

The "even if" clause is the positioning angle in one phrase. It names the thing the audience thought would stop them and dismisses it.

**Examples by framework:**

Problem-solution: "After Freelance Finance 101, you will file your quarterly taxes correctly and on time, even if you have no accounting background and your income is completely irregular."

Category creation: "After The Quiet Launch, you will fill your course enrollment without a single piece of launch content, even if you have a small audience and hate selling on social media."

Against the alternative: "After Bookkeeping for Etsy Sellers, you will hand your accountant organized, categorized records at tax time, even if you have been using a shoebox and a prayer for the past three years."

Identity transformation: "After Write Your First Novel, you will finish a complete first draft, even if you have started and abandoned three manuscripts already."
