# Course Naming Formulas

Ten formulas for naming online courses, with usage guidance and examples. Use a spread of formulas rather than defaulting to the same pattern twice.

---

## Formula 1: The Result Name

**What it does:** Names the course after the exact outcome the student achieves. No ambiguity. The name is the promise.

**When to use:** When the outcome is highly specific, measurable, and desirable enough to stand on its own. Works at any price point. Especially strong for business, finance, and fitness courses where the result is concrete.

**Guidance:** The more specific the result, the stronger the name. "Get More Clients" is weak. "First 10 Clients in 90 Days" is strong.

**Examples:**
- "Your First 10 Clients in 90 Days"
- "Zero to $5K Months: Freelance Finance for Beginners"
- "Drop 10 Pounds Before Your Next Blood Panel"

---

## Formula 2: The Method Name

**What it does:** Names the course after a proprietary method, system, or framework the creator has developed or named. Creates a distinct brand asset.

**When to use:** When the creator has a named system that students already know, or when the course teaches a specific approach that differs from common methods. Works especially well at higher price points where the "how" matters as much as the "what."

**Guidance:** The method name must sound like it belongs to the creator. Generic-sounding method names ("The 5-Step System") are weak. Named methods ("The Clarity Audit," "The Revenue Loop") are stronger. The name does not need to describe the method; it just needs to be memorable.

**Examples:**
- "The Clarity Audit: A Course for Overwhelmed Freelancers"
- "The Revenue Loop: Email Marketing for Service Providers"
- "The Deep Work Method: Focus Training for Knowledge Workers"

---

## Formula 3: The Audience Name

**What it does:** Starts with the specific audience so the right person immediately feels seen, and the wrong person self-selects out.

**When to use:** When the audience is a tight niche (real estate agents, Pilates instructors, podcast editors) and the course is designed specifically for them. The audience label becomes the differentiator.

**Guidance:** Combine the audience identifier with a result or method for maximum clarity. The audience name alone is too broad; "For Coaches" tells you who but not what.

**Examples:**
- "Bookkeeping for Etsy Sellers: Tax-Ready in 30 Days"
- "Email Marketing for Therapists: Fill Your Practice Without Paid Ads"
- "Web Design for Non-Designers: Build Your First Client Site"

---

## Formula 4: The Timeline Name

**What it does:** Names the course with a specific time frame, creating urgency and setting expectations for the transformation.

**When to use:** When the result can be achieved in a well-defined period. The timeline makes the promise feel real and achievable. Works well for anything with a defined process: 30 days, 6 weeks, 90 days.

**Guidance:** The timeline must be honest. "Six Weeks to [X]" where X takes most students 6 months will generate refunds and bad reviews. Use the timeline that reflects a realistic committed student's pace, not the creator's ideal.

**Examples:**
- "6 Weeks to Your First Paying Copywriting Client"
- "90-Day Podcast Launch: From Idea to Published"
- "30 Days to a Profitable Etsy Shop"

---

## Formula 5: The Transformation Name

**What it does:** Frames the course as a journey from a painful before state to a desirable after state. The name itself tells the story.

**When to use:** When the before state is a recognizable, specific frustration the audience would admit to. Strongest for personal development, mindset, and skill-building courses where the transformation is as important as the tactical outcome.

**Guidance:** The before state must be honest and specific, not dramatic. "From Broke to Rich" is vague and overpromises. "From Inconsistent Income to a Reliable Retainer Model" is honest and specific.

**Examples:**
- "From Undercharging to Fully Booked: Pricing for Freelancers"
- "From Overwhelmed to Organized: Productivity for Solopreneurs"
- "From Hobbyist to Hired: Building a Photography Portfolio That Books"

---

## Formula 6: The Specificity Name

**What it does:** Uses extreme specificity in the title to signal that this course is for one type of person in one type of situation, making it feel tailor-made.

**When to use:** In crowded niches where general courses already exist. The specificity is the differentiator. Also useful when selling to an audience that has tried general solutions before.

**Guidance:** The specificity must be real, not just marketing. The course must actually deliver on the specific claim in the name.

**Examples:**
- "Instagram Marketing for B2B Service Businesses (Under 5K Followers)"
- "Tax Planning for Freelance Developers Working with US Clients"
- "YouTube SEO for Channels That Have Never Hit 1,000 Subscribers"

---

## Formula 7: The Clever or Coined Name

**What it does:** Creates a memorable, original name that sticks in the mind and becomes a brand asset. May include wordplay, alliteration, a coined term, or an unexpected pairing.

**When to use:** When the creator has a strong personal brand and wants the course name to be shareable and memorable. Higher risk (the meaning is not immediately obvious) but higher reward (becomes a recognizable brand). Best for mid-to-high price points and warm audiences.

**Guidance:** The clever name must still connect to a real outcome in the tagline or subtitle. Do not be so clever that the potential student has no idea what the course teaches.

**Examples:**
- "Inbox Zero for Freelancers: Email Done by 10am"
- "The Quiet Launch: Sell Your Course Without Social Media"
- "Permission to Charge More: Rate Confidence for Service Providers"

---

## Formula 8: The Objection Name

**What it does:** Names the course after the most common objection or excuse, then promises to solve it.

**When to use:** When the target audience has a specific belief that has held them back from taking action (no time, no budget, no tech skills). Naming the objection signals: "I know what you are thinking, and this course specifically handles that."

**Guidance:** The objection must be the real reason this audience has not acted, not a surface-level one. Research or creator knowledge matters here. Combine with a result for the strongest version.

**Examples:**
- "No Budget Marketing: A Course for Bootstrapped Founders"
- "Non-Tech Course Launch: Publish Your First Course Without Touching Code"
- "The Introvert's Guide to Getting Clients: Sales Without the Cringe"

---

## Formula 9: The Insider Access Name

**What it does:** Suggests the student is getting access to something real professionals know that others do not. Creates a sense of exclusivity and insider knowledge.

**When to use:** When the creator has professional credentials or direct industry experience that most competitors lack. Works well for courses that teach industry-specific skills, behind-the-scenes knowledge, or professional practices.

**Guidance:** The insider access must be real. If the creator does not actually have insider knowledge, this formula will feel hollow. Pair with a specific benefit.

**Examples:**
- "What Casting Directors Actually Look For: Audition Prep for Working Actors"
- "Agency Pricing Decoded: What Freelancers Charge and Why"
- "The Editor's Playbook: Book Editing Skills for Aspiring Fiction Writers"

---

## Formula 10: The Challenge Name

**What it does:** Frames the course as a challenge with a defined endpoint, using action-oriented language and a clear goal. Creates momentum and commitment.

**When to use:** For courses with a strong action-orientation and a defined deliverable at the end. The "challenge" framing also works well for cohort-based or live courses where a group is moving together.

**Guidance:** The challenge must be achievable. Overclaiming ("Write Your Book in a Weekend") creates scepticism and disappointment. Concrete and real is better than impressive and implausible.

**Examples:**
- "The 21-Day Content Sprint: Post Every Day and Build Your Audience"
- "The Launch Challenge: Your Course Live in 14 Days"
- "The 30-Day Rate Reset: Raise Your Prices This Month"

## Matching naming formula to price point

| Price tier | Recommended formulas | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Under $97 | Result, Timeline, Transformation | Low price needs an obvious, immediate benefit; no mystery |
| $97-$497 | Result, Method, Audience, Specificity, Objection | Mid-price benefits from a clear system or niche specificity |
| $497-$997 | Method, Insider Access, Clever/Coined | Higher price can carry a stronger brand name with a tagline for clarity |
| $997+ | Method, Clever/Coined, Insider Access | Premium price supports a proprietary name; the brand carries the meaning |
