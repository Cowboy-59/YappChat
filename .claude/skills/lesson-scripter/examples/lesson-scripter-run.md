### HOOK

There's a moment every freelancer knows. The invoice clears, the money hits your account, and for about 48 hours you feel great. Then comes the anxiety. Did I hold back enough for taxes? Can I pay myself this month? What about that software subscription I forgot about?

That feeling is not a discipline problem. It is a systems problem. And in this lesson I'm going to give you the system that makes it stop.

[PAUSE]

---

### OBJECTIVE

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to apply the three-bucket allocation system to any payment you receive, no matter the size, so that every dollar goes exactly where it should the moment it hits your account.

---

### BEAT 1: Why traditional budgets fail freelancers

Let me start with why the typical budgeting advice does not work for us.

The advice you've probably heard is: track your expenses, set monthly spending limits, stick to them. And that advice works beautifully if your income is the same every month. The problem is yours is not.

One month you invoice $2,200. The next you invoice $6,000. The month after that, you invoice $900 because a client was slow and a project pushed. A monthly budget assumes a number that does not exist for most freelancers.

[PAUSE]

So instead of a monthly budget, we use a per-payment system. Every time money comes in, you allocate it immediately. You do not wait until the end of the month to see where things landed. You decide now.

That is what the three-bucket system does.

---

### BEAT 2: The three buckets

There are three buckets, and every dollar you earn goes into one of them.

[SHOW: simple diagram with three labeled buckets: TAX, OPERATING, PERSONAL]

Bucket one is TAX. This is the money you will owe at the end of the year. You set it aside immediately and you do not touch it. I'll tell you the percentage in a moment.

Bucket two is OPERATING. This covers everything it costs to run your business: software, tools, professional development, any contractors or part-time help. Think of this as the cost of staying in business.

Bucket three is PERSONAL. This is what you actually pay yourself. It goes into a separate personal account and becomes your living expenses: rent, food, everything outside the business.

[PAUSE]

Three buckets. Three accounts. When money comes in, it gets split. When it is split, you always know exactly what you have.

---

### BEAT 3: The starting percentages

Now, I said I would give you the percentages. Here they are.

[SHOW: percentage breakdown graphic]

- Tax bucket: 25-30% of every payment. Use 30% if you are in the US and earning above $50,000 as a self-employed person; 25% if you are below that. If you are in a different country, ask your accountant for the right number and replace it here. The principle is the same.
- Operating bucket: 15-20% of every payment. Start at 15% if you have low operating costs; move to 20% if you have employees, contractors, or high software expenses.
- Personal bucket: what's left. For most freelancers starting out, that is 50-60%.

These are starting percentages. They are not a rule. We will look at how to adjust them in Lesson 2.4. For now, use them exactly as I've given them.

[PAUSE]

---

### BEAT 4: A fully worked example

Let me show you exactly how this works with real numbers.

Imagine Sam, a freelance video editor, two years in. Sam just received payment for a project: $1,800.

Here is what Sam does the moment that payment clears.

Step 1: Tax bucket. Sam takes 30% of $1,800. That is $540. Sam transfers $540 to the account labeled "Tax." It does not exist anymore for spending purposes. It is already owed.

Step 2: Operating bucket. Sam has monthly software costs and occasionally pays a contractor. Sam uses 15%, which is $270. That goes into the "Operating" account.

Step 3: Personal. What's left is $1,800 minus $540 minus $270: $990. That goes into Sam's personal account and becomes Sam's paycheck for this payment.

[PAUSE]

Notice what Sam did not do. Sam did not think about whether $990 was enough. Sam did not debate whether to hold back more for tax. Sam followed the system. The system made the decision. Sam just transferred the money.

That is the whole point. A system that works is one you do not have to think about.

[SHOW: before/after comparison: "End of the year with no system: guessing what you owe in taxes." vs "End of the year with three buckets: tax account has exactly what you owe."]

---

### SUMMARY

Let's recap what we covered.

Traditional monthly budgets do not work for irregular income. The three-bucket system solves that by making a per-payment decision, every time, with fixed percentages.

The three buckets are Tax, Operating, and Personal. The percentages are 25-30% tax, 15-20% operating, and the rest is personal.

[PAUSE]

The single most important thing I want you to take from this lesson: the system only works if you apply it immediately, when the payment clears. Not at the end of the month. Not when you get around to it. The moment the money arrives.
