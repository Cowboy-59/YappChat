# Student Email Map

A complete map of every email touchpoint in the student lifecycle, from purchase through course graduation. Includes purpose, timing, content outline, subject line examples, and notes for email platform setup.

---

## Email 1: Welcome (Day 0, Immediate)

**Trigger:** Purchase confirmed.
**Purpose:** Deliver access, celebrate the decision, set the expectation for what happens next.
**Tone:** Warm, direct, brief. This email is usually read on a phone within minutes of purchase. Not the place for long copy.

**Content outline:**
1. Acknowledge the purchase: "You're in."
2. One sentence on what they can look forward to.
3. The single most important next step: "Start here: [link to Module 1 or the Getting Started page]."
4. How to get help: "If you have any issues with access, reply to this email."

**Subject line options:**
- "You're in. Here's your first step."
- "Welcome to [Course Name]. Start here."
- "[Course Name] access is ready."

**Length:** Under 200 words.

**Platform note:** Most course platforms send a default purchase confirmation email. If the platform does this, the Day 0 email should be a separate, personal welcome from the creator, not a duplicate of the platform's automated receipt.

---

## Email 2: How to Get Started (Day 2)

**Trigger:** Time-based (2 days after purchase).
**Purpose:** For students who have not yet logged in or started, reduce friction by telling them exactly what to do first and why the first module is worth starting today.

**Content outline:**
1. Acknowledge that life gets busy.
2. One sentence on the payoff waiting in Module 1 (the specific thing they will learn or do).
3. "Start by doing one thing: [specific action, e.g., 'watch the 12-minute intro video in Module 1']."
4. Optional: share one student insight or quick win from the module to make it feel real.

**Subject line options:**
- "Still thinking about starting? This will help."
- "The first step is shorter than you think."
- "One thing to do today: [specific module action]."

**Length:** 150-250 words.

---

## Email 3: Commitment and Context (Day 5-7)

**Trigger:** Time-based (5-7 days after purchase).
**Purpose:** Help the student understand how to fit the course into their real life. Give them a simple plan for when and how to make progress.

**Content outline:**
1. One honest statement about course completion: "Most people who don't finish courses get stuck at the 'I'll do it when I have time' stage."
2. Ask the student to make a simple commitment: "Set one block of time this week to complete Module [X]. Even 45 minutes is enough."
3. Optional: a 4-week or 6-week completion schedule for the course (one module per week or similar).
4. Reminder that the community (if applicable) is a good place to post their progress.

**Subject line options:**
- "Make one commitment this week."
- "When do you plan to finish [Course Name]?"
- "A 4-week plan if you want it."

**Length:** 200-300 words.

---

## Email 4: Mid-Course Check-In (At the halfway point)

**Trigger:** Time-based (at the halfway point of the expected course duration, e.g., Week 3 of a 6-week expected completion).
**Purpose:** Re-engage students who have slowed down or gone quiet. Acknowledge the normal friction of the middle stretch.

**Content outline:**
1. Acknowledge the reality: "If you've slowed down, you're in good company."
2. Name what's coming in the second half of the course and why it's worth getting there.
3. One clear ask: "Which module are you currently on? Reply and let me know, or find your next step here: [link]."

**Subject line options:**
- "Checking in. Where are you in the course?"
- "The halfway point is the hardest. Here's what's on the other side."
- "[Specific module or concept] is the part most students say changed things."

**Length:** 150-200 words.

---

## Email 5: Drop-Off Nudge (15-21 days of inactivity)

**Trigger:** Platform activity trigger (student has not logged in or progressed in 15-21 days). If the email platform does not support activity-based triggers, this becomes a time-based send.
**Purpose:** Re-engage without guilt. The student is not failing; they just need a gentle re-entry point.

**Content outline:**
1. Brief, warm acknowledgment: "Hey, I just wanted to check in."
2. No guilt language. No "you've been missing," "you haven't logged in," or "don't let this go to waste."
3. One specific re-entry point: "If you want to pick back up, [Module X] is a good place to re-start. It takes about [time]."
4. Optional: a small piece of value (a tip or insight) to re-warm the relationship before asking them to act.

**Subject line options:**
- "Still here if you need me."
- "A good place to pick back up."
- "No pressure, but I wanted to check in."

**Length:** 100-150 words. Brevity is the point. This email should feel like a text from a friend, not a marketing campaign.

---

## Email 6: Final Module Nudge (80-90% complete, no activity)

**Trigger:** Platform activity trigger (student has completed all but the final 1-2 modules and has gone inactive for 7-10 days).
**Purpose:** Address the "proximity effect": students who stop just before the finish line because almost done feels close enough to done.

**Content outline:**
1. Name their progress specifically: "You've completed [X] of [Y] modules."
2. Tell them how close they are: "The final module takes about [time]."
3. Explain what they will have when they finish: the graduation moment, the certificate if applicable, the specific capability the final module delivers.
4. CTA: "Finish it today."

**Subject line options:**
- "You're [X] minutes away from finishing."
- "Almost there. Here's the last step."
- "One module left."

---

## Email 7: Completion and Graduation

**Trigger:** Course marked complete on the platform.
**Purpose:** Celebrate the student, acknowledge the transformation, invite a testimonial, offer the next step.

**Content outline:**
1. Celebrate specifically: "You finished [Course Name]. That means you now [specific capability from the course]."
2. Acknowledge what it took: "That wasn't nothing. Most people who buy a course like this don't finish it. You did."
3. Optional: share what this accomplishment means in the larger context of their goal.
4. Testimonial invitation: "If you'd be willing to share what the course meant for you, I'd be grateful. A sentence or two is plenty. Reply to this email."
5. Next step: "If you're ready to go further, here's [related resource / next course / community link]."

**Subject line options:**
- "You finished [Course Name]. Congratulations."
- "You did it. What comes next."
- "Course complete. Here's what's next for you."

**Length:** 250-350 words.

---

## Platform and segmentation notes

**Teachable, Thinkific, Kajabi:** All three platforms have built-in email triggers for purchase, module completion, and course completion. Use the native triggers for Emails 1, milestone celebrations, and Email 7. Use an external email platform (Klaviyo, Mailchimp, ConvertKit) for the time-based sequences (Emails 2, 3, 4) and inactivity nudges (Emails 5, 6) if the course platform's native automation is limited.

**Klaviyo:** Use a post-purchase flow triggered by a tag or purchase event. Set delays as draft. Never schedule or send without explicit creator confirmation. Use the flow's conditional split to branch students who have completed the course (via platform webhook or tag) away from nudge sequences.

**Segmentation:** If the email list contains both current students and past students or subscribers, tag course buyers at purchase and ensure nudge sequences only go to current enrolled students. Sending a "how's the course going?" email to someone who completed it six months ago creates confusion and erodes trust.
