# Course Sales Page Structure

A complete section-by-section blueprint for writing a course sales page that converts browsers into enrolled students. Each section has a conversion role, a recommended length, and example copy blocks.

---

## Section 1: Hero (Headline + Sub-headline + Opening)

**Conversion role:** Stop the scroll. Confirm the reader is in the right place within 5 seconds.

**Length:** Headline (8-15 words), sub-headline (15-25 words), opening paragraph (3-4 sentences).

**What makes it work:**
- The headline names the outcome or the frustration, not the course.
- The sub-headline adds who it is for and what they leave with.
- The opening paragraph sounds like the creator talking to a real person, not a press release.

**Headline patterns that work for courses:**

Pattern 1 (Result): "Finally, [specific outcome], even if [the objection they've had]."
Example: "Finally, consistent freelance clients, even if you hate selling."

Pattern 2 (Frustration-first): "If you've been [doing thing] and still [not getting result], here's why."
Example: "If you've been posting every day and still not growing your list, here's why."

Pattern 3 (Direct promise): "Learn [specific skill] in [timeframe]. No [thing they dread]."
Example: "Learn hand-building pottery in 30 days. No wheel, no studio, no prior experience."

Pattern 4 (Transformation): "From [before state] to [after state]: A course for [audience]."
Example: "From first idea to published course: A step-by-step program for first-time course creators."

**Example opening paragraph:**
"You have been doing [thing they do]. You have read the advice. You have tried the [solution they've attempted]. And you are still [stuck state]. This course exists because that is a solvable problem, and because the fix is usually simpler than the advice makes it sound."

## Section 2: Empathy ("You Might Be Here")

**Conversion role:** Make the reader feel deeply understood. Demonstrate that the creator knows their situation without having to be told.

**Length:** Short intro line, then 4-6 bullet points.

**What makes it work:**
- Bullets describe specific situations, not vague feelings.
- They name the thoughts the reader has at 10pm when they are frustrated, not polished business-speak versions of their problems.
- The best empathy bullets make the reader think "How did they know that?"

**Example format:**
"If any of this sounds familiar, you're in the right place:"

- "You have started [thing] three times and never finished because [specific reason]."
- "You Googled [problem] and got 47 contradictory answers, none of which applied to your situation."
- "You have been meaning to [action] for six months but something always comes up first."
- "You see other people in your field doing [thing] and wonder what they know that you don't."
- "You know the outcome you want but you don't know the exact sequence to get there."

---

## Section 3: Course Introduction

**Conversion role:** Name and describe the course with confidence. This is the pivot from problem to solution.

**Length:** 2-3 short paragraphs.

**What makes it work:**
- State the course name, the format (self-paced, 6-week live, etc.), and the promise in the first sentence.
- Brief description of the approach or method that makes it different.
- One sentence about who built it and why (full creator bio comes later).

**Example format:**
"[Course name] is a [format] course that [course promise in one sentence].

It is built around [the core method or approach]. Instead of [what competing approaches do], this course [what makes it different in concrete terms].

It was built by [creator's relevant credential in one phrase] for [specific audience description]."

---

## Section 4: What's Inside (Modules and Bonuses)

**Conversion role:** Justify the price. Show that the content is substantive and sequenced.

**Length:** Module list with one-line descriptions, then bonus list.

**Module format:**

**Module [Number]: [Module Name]**
[One sentence describing what the student learns.] By the end of this module, you will [specific takeaway or capability].

**What makes module descriptions work:**
- Lead with the outcome, not the topic.
- Include the "aha moment" where possible: the thing that clicks for the student.
- Avoid dry table of contents language. "In this module, we cover..." is weak. "This is where..." or "By the end of this module, you will..." is stronger.

**Bonus format:**

**Bonus: [Bonus Name]** (value: $[XX])
[One sentence on what it is and why it matters. Be specific.]

Recommended language for bonuses:
"This bonus alone will save you [time or money or pain] because [specific reason]."
Avoid: "An incredible resource that adds massive value."

---

## Section 5: Creator Credibility

**Conversion role:** Give the reader a reason to trust this particular person to teach this particular thing.

**Length:** 2 short paragraphs, plus an optional credential list.

**What makes it work:**
- Lead with the relevant credential or experience, not with the creator's general life story.
- Include one specific result the creator has achieved that is directly relevant to what they teach.
- Optional: a short list of credentials, client types, publications, or media mentions (without padding).
- End with something personal: why they teach this, in one sentence.

**What to avoid:**
- Listing every certification and degree unrelated to the course topic.
- Third-person bios ("Dr. [Name] is an award-winning...").
- Vague claims ("has helped thousands").

---

## Section 6: Social Proof

**Conversion role:** Reduce risk by showing that real people have gotten results.

**Testimonial format (ideal):**
"[Two to four sentences describing a specific result or experience the student had, in their own words.]"
- [First name, last name or first name + role/situation], [optional: timeframe since taking the course]

**What makes testimonials convert:**
- Specific results beat general praise. "This course helped me finally understand X" beats "This was amazing!"
- The testimonial author's situation should mirror the reader's: a beginner who took it, not a professional who already knew the material.
- If possible, include the student's before state in the testimonial.

**When no testimonials exist yet:**

Option 1: "Beta student preview" language:
"This course is currently in its founding-student round. Here is what the first cohort said after completing Module 1: [genuine early feedback]."

Option 2: Social proof bridge:
"By the end of this course, students consistently report [specific experience]. Here is what that looks like in practice: [scenario or outcome description]."

Do not fabricate testimonials or use placeholder names.

## Section 7: FAQ and Objection Handling

**Conversion role:** Remove the last obstacles to buying. Acknowledge that the reader has real questions.

**Length:** 5-8 questions with concise, honest answers.

**Standard objections to address for most courses:**

"How long will this take me?"
Answer by giving the total content hours plus a realistic weekly time commitment. Be honest about whether the student can do it in parallel with a full-time job.

"I'm not sure this will work for me because [niche situation]."
Address the most common niche objection directly. If the course works for people in that situation, explain why.

"I've tried similar things before and they didn't work."
Acknowledge that other approaches have failed them. Explain specifically what is different about this course's approach and why that difference matters.

"What if I fall behind or can't finish in time?"
Address access policy. If the course is self-paced with lifetime access, say so clearly and simply.

"What if I don't like it?"
State the refund policy plainly. "If you complete Modules 1-3 and are not satisfied, contact us within 30 days for a full refund." Be specific; "satisfaction guarantee" without conditions is legally ambiguous and reduces credibility.

"Is this for beginners or do I need experience?"
State the experience level required plainly. List any prerequisites. If none are required, say "none."

## Section 8: Price, Value Stack, and CTA

**Conversion role:** Make the price feel like a clear, justified yes.

**Value stack format:**

What you get:
- [Course Name] (full course, [X] modules, [format]): [stated value, $XXX]
- [Bonus 1 name]: [stated value, $XXX]
- [Bonus 2 name]: [stated value, $XXX]
- Total value: [$XXXX]

Today's price: $[XX] or [X] payments of $[XX]

**Principles for value stacks:**
- Stated values should be defensible. If the bonus is a PDF worksheet, $97 is probably too high. If it is a live coaching call, $200 is reasonable.
- Do not compare the course price to absurd alternatives ("this is less than a gym membership") unless the comparison is genuinely relevant and respectful.
- If there is a payment plan, present it clearly and without buried fees.

**CTA button copy principles:**
- Make the action specific, not generic. "Start Module 1 Today" beats "Buy Now."
- Use the course name or the result. "Join Pottery for People Who Don't Have a Wheel" or "Get Instant Access."
- If there is urgency (cart closing, enrollment limit), state it plainly and honestly. Do not manufacture false urgency.

---

## Section 9: Closing Paragraph

**Conversion role:** Speak to the reader who made it to the bottom but hasn't clicked yet.

**Length:** 3-5 sentences.

**What makes it work:**
- Acknowledge the decision. "You've made it this far, which means you already know the outcome you want."
- Restate the promise in one sentence.
- Give a clear, confident next step.
- No final pressure. The best closing copy trusts the reader to make the right call for themselves.

**Example:**
"If you have read this far, you already know whether this course is for you. The only question is whether now is the right time to act on it. [Course promise restated in one sentence.] If you are ready, the first module is waiting. If you are not, bookmark this page and come back when you are."
