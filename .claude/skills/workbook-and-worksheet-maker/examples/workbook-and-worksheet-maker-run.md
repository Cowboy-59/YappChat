### Lesson 2.1 Worksheet: Why Centering Fails

**What this lesson covered:**
Centering fails for two reasons almost every time: incorrect arm position (arms not braced against the body) and inconsistent pressure (students push with varying force instead of steady, sustained pressure). Fixing the position makes the pressure easier to manage.

### Lesson 2.2 Worksheet: The Body-Position Reset

**What this lesson covered:**
The body-position reset is a pre-throw checklist: feet flat and stable, seat bone on the edge of the stool (not leaning back), elbows anchored to your hips or inner thighs, and one hand on top of the clay with the other on the side. This position makes centering a full-body action, not just an arm action.

### Lesson 2.4 Worksheet: Opening the Centered Clay

**What this lesson covered:**
Opening transforms centered clay into a vessel. The two-step opening process: (1) press your thumb into the center of the clay to start the hole, and (2) use two or three fingers to widen the floor to your target wall thickness, stopping at about a quarter inch from the bat. The most common mistake is opening too fast, which destabilizes the centered clay.

**Your exercise:**
