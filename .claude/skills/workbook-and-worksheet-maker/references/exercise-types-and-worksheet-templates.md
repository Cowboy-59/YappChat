# Exercise Types and Worksheet Templates

Full taxonomy of exercise types, when to use each, and ten ready-to-adapt worksheet templates with labeled output placeholders.

---

## 1. Exercise type taxonomy

### Type 1: Recall exercise

**What it is:** The student retrieves information from the lesson without looking at their notes.
**When to use it:** Concept lessons where vocabulary or a framework must be retained before the student can apply it.
**Output:** A list, a filled-in diagram, or a completed definition table.
**Caution:** Recall alone is the lowest-value exercise type. Use it sparingly and only as a gateway to an application exercise.

**Template prompt:** "Without referring to the lesson, [list / define / label] the [FRAMEWORK ELEMENTS / TERMS / STEPS]. Then check your answers against the lesson."

---

### Type 2: Application exercise

**What it is:** The student applies a method, framework, or skill to a provided scenario or to their own situation.
**When to use it:** After concept and demonstration lessons. This is the highest-frequency exercise type in a good workbook.
**Output:** A completed artifact using the provided method (a filled-in template, a calculation, a decision matrix, a drafted piece).
**Quality test:** Can the student complete this exercise using only what was taught in the lesson? If they need information from outside the course, the exercise is either too complex or too underspecified.

---

### Type 3: Analysis exercise

**What it is:** The student examines a provided piece of work (a case, an example, a scenario) and identifies what is strong, what is weak, and why.
**When to use it:** After worked example lessons and in the middle of a module, once the student has enough context to evaluate quality.
**Output:** An annotated example, a comparison table, or a written critique with reasons.

**Template prompt:** "Review the [EXAMPLE BELOW / ATTACHED CASE]. Using the criteria from this lesson, identify: (1) what is done well and why, (2) what could be improved and how."

---

### Type 4: Creation exercise

**What it is:** The student builds an original artifact from scratch or from a template.
**When to use it:** Practice and synthesis lessons. This is the highest-value exercise type: the output is something the student can use in their real work.
**Output:** A fully produced artifact (a drafted paragraph, a completed plan, a built model, a designed template).
**Quality test:** The output should be specific enough to this student's situation that it would not look identical to any other student's output. Generic outputs suggest the prompt is too open or too vague.

---

### Type 5: Reflection exercise

**What it is:** The student considers how the lesson connects to their own situation, history, or goals.
**When to use it:** At the end of each lesson worksheet and as the module-level reflection. Reflection without application is incomplete; always pair it with a prior application exercise.
**Output:** A few written sentences or bullet points that are personal, not generic.
**Quality test:** If every student's reflection could read identically, the prompt is too abstract. Add specificity: "In your own [PROJECT / WORK / BUSINESS], where have you seen [CONCEPT FROM LESSON] play out?"

---

### Type 6: Planning exercise

**What it is:** The student makes a decision and commits to a specific action in a specific timeframe.
**When to use it:** At the end of modules, as the module progress commitment, or in habit-formation courses.
**Output:** A specific plan: what, when, how, any obstacles anticipated.
**Template structure:** "I will [SPECIFIC ACTION] by [DATE/TIMEFRAME]. The one obstacle I anticipate is [OBSTACLE]. My plan to handle it is [RESPONSE]."

---

### Type 7: Comparison exercise

**What it is:** The student places two or more things side by side and identifies how they differ across defined criteria.
**When to use it:** When the lesson teaches a distinction (concept A vs concept B, the old approach vs the new approach, example 1 vs example 2).
**Output:** A completed comparison table or a written analysis with at least three comparison dimensions.

---

### Type 8: Diagnosis exercise

**What it is:** The student evaluates their current situation, practice, or output against a defined standard and identifies the gap.
**When to use it:** At the beginning of a module (as the Before You Start prompt) or when the course teaches an audit or assessment skill.
**Output:** A self-assessment score, a gap analysis, or a prioritized list of areas to address.
**Template:** "Rate your current approach to [TOPIC] on each dimension below (1-5). Then identify the one area where improvement would have the biggest impact."

---

## 2. Ten ready-to-adapt worksheet templates

---

### Template 1: Concept application (most common)

**Lesson type:** Concept, demonstration.

---
**Lesson [X.X]: [LESSON TITLE]**

**What this lesson covered:**
[One to two sentence recap of the core idea. Example: "The three-bucket allocation system assigns every dollar you earn to one of three categories: tax, operating, or personal. Splitting immediately prevents the end-of-year tax surprise."]

**Your exercise:**
Apply the [METHOD/FRAMEWORK] to your own situation. Fill in the table below.

| [COLUMN 1 LABEL] | [COLUMN 2 LABEL] | [COLUMN 3 LABEL] |
|---|---|---|
| [STUDENT FILLS IN] | [STUDENT FILLS IN] | [STUDENT FILLS IN] |
| [STUDENT FILLS IN] | [STUDENT FILLS IN] | [STUDENT FILLS IN] |
| [STUDENT FILLS IN] | [STUDENT FILLS IN] | [STUDENT FILLS IN] |

**Reflection:**
What surprised you about your answers? Where in your current [WORK/PROJECT/BUSINESS] does this apply most directly?

[STUDENT FILLS IN: 2-3 sentences]

---

### Template 2: Before-and-after rewrite

**Lesson type:** Demonstration, worked example.

---
**Lesson [X.X]: [LESSON TITLE]**

**What this lesson covered:**
[One to two sentence recap.]

**Your exercise:**
Below is a [PIECE OF CONTENT / DESCRIPTION / VERSION] that has a common problem. Using the method from this lesson, identify the problem and rewrite it.

**Original:**
[EXAMPLE TEXT OR SCENARIO, STUDENT READS AND EVALUATES]

**The problem I identified:**
[STUDENT FILLS IN: one or two sentences]

**My revised version:**
[STUDENT FILLS IN: rewritten version]

**What changed and why:**
[STUDENT FILLS IN: one or two sentences explaining the key decision]

---

### Template 3: Decision matrix

**Lesson type:** Analysis, planning.

---
**Lesson [X.X]: [LESSON TITLE]**

**What this lesson covered:**
[One to two sentence recap.]

**Your exercise:**
Use the decision matrix below to evaluate [DECISION / OPTIONS / APPROACHES] in your own situation.

| Option | [CRITERION 1] (1-5) | [CRITERION 2] (1-5) | [CRITERION 3] (1-5) | Total |
|--------|---|---|---|---|
| [OPTION A] | | | | |
| [OPTION B] | | | | |
| [OPTION C] | | | | |

**My recommendation:**
Based on the scores above, I will [DECISION] because [STUDENT FILLS IN: one or two sentences].

**One potential downside I will watch for:**
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

---

### Template 4: Annotated example

**Lesson type:** Analysis, worked example.

---
**Lesson [X.X]: [LESSON TITLE]**

**What this lesson covered:**
[One to two sentence recap.]

**Your exercise:**
Read the example below. Annotate it using the labels provided.

[EXAMPLE TEXT, SCENARIO, OR IMAGE DESCRIPTION]

Label each element:
- Mark with a "S" where the example is **strong** according to this lesson's criteria.
- Mark with a "W" where the example is **weak** or could be improved.
- Write a one-sentence note for each "W" explaining what you would change.

**My annotations:**
[STUDENT FILLS IN: annotated copy or bullet-point notes]

**The single most important change I would make:**
[STUDENT FILLS IN: one sentence]

---

### Template 5: Sentence starter (low friction for beginners)

**Lesson type:** Concept, reflection. Use when the student needs significant scaffolding.

---
**Lesson [X.X]: [LESSON TITLE]**

**What this lesson covered:**
[One to two sentence recap.]

**Your exercise:**
Complete each sentence below based on your own situation.

The biggest challenge I currently have with [TOPIC] is:
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

The approach from this lesson that most applies to my situation is:
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

The one thing I will try first is:
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

One concern I have about applying this is:
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

---

### Template 6: Create-from-template

**Lesson type:** Practice, creation.

---
**Lesson [X.X]: [LESSON TITLE]**

**What this lesson covered:**
[One to two sentence recap.]

**Your exercise:**
Use the template below to build your own [DELIVERABLE].

**[DELIVERABLE NAME] Template:**

[SECTION 1 LABEL]:
[STUDENT FILLS IN: instructions for this section]

[SECTION 2 LABEL]:
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

[SECTION 3 LABEL]:
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

[SECTION 4 LABEL] (optional):
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

**After completing the template, answer this:**
Is there any section where you were unsure what to write? Note it here so you can revisit the relevant lesson beat.
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

---

### Template 7: Process trace

**Lesson type:** Synthesis, demonstration.

---
**Lesson [X.X]: [LESSON TITLE]**

**What this lesson covered:**
[One to two sentence recap.]

**Your exercise:**
Walk through the [PROCESS / METHOD] from this lesson, applied to your own [PROJECT / SITUATION]. Document each step as you go.

| Step | What I did | Key decision I made | Result or output |
|------|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| 1. [STEP NAME] | [STUDENT FILLS IN] | [STUDENT FILLS IN] | [STUDENT FILLS IN] |
| 2. [STEP NAME] | | | |
| 3. [STEP NAME] | | | |
| 4. [STEP NAME] | | | |

**What I would do differently next time:**
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

---

### Template 8: Gap analysis

**Lesson type:** Diagnosis, analysis.

---
**Lesson [X.X]: [LESSON TITLE]**

**What this lesson covered:**
[One to two sentence recap.]

**Your exercise:**
Rate your current practice on each criterion below. Then identify the highest-priority gap.

| Criterion | Current score (1-5) | What "5" would look like |
|-----------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| [CRITERION 1] | | [DESCRIPTION OF EXCELLENT PERFORMANCE] |
| [CRITERION 2] | | |
| [CRITERION 3] | | |
| [CRITERION 4] | | |

**My highest-priority gap:**
[CRITERION]: I scored it [SCORE] because [STUDENT FILLS IN: one or two sentences].

**My first action to close this gap:**
[STUDENT FILLS IN: specific, completable in under a week]

---

### Template 9: Synthesis capstone

**Lesson type:** Module synthesis. This integrates outputs from earlier worksheets.

---
**Module [N] Capstone: [MODULE DELIVERABLE NAME]**

**What this exercise builds:**
This is the completion marker for Module [N]. Using your outputs from Lessons [X.X], [X.X], and [X.X], you will produce [DELIVERABLE DESCRIPTION].

**Gather your inputs:**
- From Lesson [X.X] worksheet: your [OUTPUT A].
- From Lesson [X.X] worksheet: your [OUTPUT B].
- From Lesson [X.X] worksheet: your [OUTPUT C].

**Build your [DELIVERABLE]:**
Using those inputs, complete the following:

[DELIVERABLE SECTION 1]:
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

[DELIVERABLE SECTION 2]:
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

[DELIVERABLE SECTION 3]:
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

[DELIVERABLE SECTION 4] (optional):
[STUDENT FILLS IN]

**Your quality check:**
Before moving to Module [N+1], confirm:
- [ ] I used outputs from at least two earlier worksheets.
- [ ] My [DELIVERABLE] is specific to my own [SITUATION / PROJECT / BUSINESS], not a generic example.
- [ ] I could explain this [DELIVERABLE] to someone else in under two minutes.

---

### Template 10: Commitment and next step

**Lesson type:** Module progress commitment. Always the last page of a module workbook.

---
**My Commitment for This Week**

This module covered: [ONE-SENTENCE MODULE SUMMARY].

My biggest takeaway: [STUDENT FILLS IN]

Before I start Module [N+1], I will:
[SPECIFIC ACTION, STUDENT FILLS IN]

By (date or timeframe): [STUDENT FILLS IN]

One obstacle I anticipate: [STUDENT FILLS IN]

How I will handle it: [STUDENT FILLS IN]

Signed: _________________ Date: _________________

*(The signature line is optional. For some students it increases commitment. For others it feels formal. Include it by default; remove it if your audience or voice suggests it.)*
