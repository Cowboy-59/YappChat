# Launch Runway for Courses

Complete frameworks for running a course enrollment launch across three phases: pre-launch warm-up, open cart, and urgency close. Includes timing models by list size, content-beat templates, urgency strategies, and post-launch review.

---

## The Three-Phase Launch Model

A course launch is not one announcement. It is a structured sequence that moves a potential student from "aware of the course" to "enrolled." The three phases each have a different job:

| Phase | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-launch warm-up | Build intent before the sales conversation | 7-14 days |
| Open cart | Convert intent into enrollment | 5-7 days |
| Urgency close | Activate the fence-sitters before time runs out | 24-48 hours |

Skipping the pre-launch phase is the single most common reason a launch underperforms. Sending a sales email to a list that has not heard from the creator in two weeks is starting cold. The warm-up does not have to be elaborate; it just has to be present.

## Pre-Launch Warm-Up Frameworks

### The "Teach First" Warm-Up

Best for: creators with an audience that responds to educational content.

Structure: 3-5 pieces of content (email, social, or both) that teach one concept related to the course topic for free. Each piece of content is valuable on its own and also makes the case, indirectly, for why the full course is worth having.

**Beat 1: The Problem Reframe**
Job: make the audience aware of a problem they may have been tolerating.
Format: "Most people think [X is the problem]. It's actually [Y]."
Example topic for a productivity course: "The reason your to-do list never gets shorter isn't discipline. It's environment."

**Beat 2: The Insight Share**
Job: give one actionable idea that creates trust and demonstrates the creator's depth.
Format: "Here's one thing I changed that made the biggest difference: [specific thing]."

**Beat 3: The Story**
Job: connect emotionally. The creator shares a moment that mirrors the audience's experience.
Format: "I remember when [specific situation]. Here's what changed it for me."

**Beat 4: The Belief Challenge**
Job: surface and pre-handle the most common objection before the sales conversation begins.
Format: "One thing that stops most [audience] from [outcome] is the belief that [common wrong belief]. Here's why that's not true."

**Beat 5: The Tease**
Job: announce that something is coming, build anticipation, invite the audience to signal interest.
Format: "This week I'm opening enrollment for [course name]. If you've been [frustrated by X], I built this for you. I'll share details on [day]."

---

### The "Waitlist" Warm-Up

Best for: creators who have a waitlist or want to segment their audience.

Before opening the full cart, open enrollment to waitlist members only for 24-48 hours. This creates:
- Early enrollments that build confidence.
- Social proof for the public launch ("over X students enrolled in the first 24 hours" is accurate and compelling).
- An incentive to join the waitlist for future launches.

**Waitlist sequence:**
- Day -3: "I'm opening a waitlist for [course name]. Here's what it gets you." (Early access + possible bonus.)
- Day -1: "Waitlist closes in 24 hours. Here's what you get as a founding student."
- Cart open day: "Enrollment is open now, starting with waitlist members."

## Open Cart Email Sequence

### 5-Day Open Cart (Standard)

**Day 1: Cart Open**
Job: announce, deliver the promise, link to the sales page.
Subject line options:
- "[Course name] is open. Here's what's inside."
- "Enrollment is open. [One-sentence promise]."
- "You can now enroll in [course name]."
Content: brief recap of who it is for, the promise, what's inside (one paragraph), the CTA, and any early-bird or founding-student bonus deadline.

**Day 2: The Story**
Job: connect the course to a real human outcome. Often best delivered as a student story or the creator's own before/after.
Subject line options:
- "[Student name] hired their first employee during this course."
- "What changed for [audience type] who went through this."
- "The moment this clicked for me."
Content: one story, told specifically. Link to sales page at the bottom. Do not re-pitch in the body; let the story do the work.

**Day 3: The Value or Objection Email**
Job: add value or handle the biggest objection. This email has the most room to be useful.
Subject line options:
- "The question I get most about [topic]."
- "Is [course] right for you? Here's a way to decide."
- "Common hesitation: I don't have time. Here's what I'd say."
Content: address one objection directly, honestly, and with specificity. End with the CTA.

**Day 4: The Scarcity or Bonus Reminder**
Job: note any real deadlines approaching. If a founding-student bonus expires, note it here.
Subject line options:
- "[Bonus name] expires tomorrow."
- "Founding student pricing ends [day]."
- "Quick reminder before [thing] expires."
Content: brief, direct. What is expiring, when, and the CTA. No padding.

**Day 5: Cart Close (Final 24 Hours)**
Job: final send before the cart closes. Creates legitimate urgency.
Subject line options:
- "[Course name] closes tonight."
- "Last chance to enroll (closes at midnight [timezone])."
- "A few hours left."
Content: brief. Recap the promise in one sentence. State the exact close time. CTA. Optional: answer one last objection in one paragraph.

---

### 7-Day Open Cart (Extended)

Use for higher-priced courses ($497+) or when the audience needs more time to decide.

Add to the 5-day sequence:
- Day 4 (additional): A detailed module spotlight. Pick the most impressive or most transformative module and write 3-4 paragraphs about what the student actually does and learns. Link to sales page.
- Day 6: A FAQ email. Pull 3-4 real questions from replies or DMs during the launch and answer them. This feels personal and builds trust.

## Urgency Close Frameworks

### Real Urgency (Always Prefer This)

Real urgency comes from:
- A literal deadline (cart closes at midnight).
- A price increase after launch week.
- A bonus that expires (founding-student call, live Q&A, extra resource).
- Limited enrollment spots (only valid if the spots are actually limited, e.g., a cohort with a live component).

**The final 24-hour email:**
Subject: "[Course name] closes in 24 hours."
Content: state the exact close time, timezone, and what the student loses by not enrolling (access to the bonuses, the founding price, or the course itself if it goes evergreen). One clear CTA. No new information needed; the reader has seen the sales page. This email just moves the fence-sitter.

**The final-hours email (2-6 hours before close):**
Subject: "Closing in a few hours."
Content: 3-4 sentences. The close time. The promise. The link. Done.

---

### When No Real Urgency Exists

If the course is evergreen and there is no genuine deadline, do not manufacture one. Instead, use:

**Commitment urgency:** "The best time to start [outcome] is before [natural life event]. If [situation] is already happening, now is the logical moment."

**Opportunity framing:** "This cohort/launch window is the only time I'm actively supporting new students through the course with [community / live calls / extra access]. After this, the course is available but the launch support ends."

**Personal deadline:** "I'm closing this launch on [date] so I can focus on building the next module / running the live cohort / other reason. After that, the page comes down until [vague future date]." Only use this if it is true.

## Timing Models by List Size

| List size | Expected enrollment rate | Minimum pre-launch days | Emails in open cart |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 500 | 2-5% | 5 days | 3-4 |
| 500-2,000 | 1-3% | 7 days | 4-5 |
| 2,000-10,000 | 0.5-2% | 10 days | 5-7 |
| 10,000+ | 0.3-1.5% | 14 days | 5-7 |

Note: These are general ranges and vary significantly by niche, price point, list warmth, and how recently the creator has launched. A warm list that hears from the creator weekly will convert at the higher end. A cold list that has not received an email in three months will convert at the lower end or below.

## Post-Launch Debrief Checklist

Complete within 48 hours of cart close:

- [ ] Total enrollments vs. goal. What was the conversion rate (enrollments / emails sent to the open cart sequence)?
- [ ] Which email had the highest click-through rate? What made it work?
- [ ] Which email had the highest unsubscribe rate? Was the subject or content off?
- [ ] Any objections that came up repeatedly in replies or DMs that the sales page should address?
- [ ] Did any bonuses drive a noticeable spike in enrollments? Which ones?
- [ ] What would you change about the pre-launch warm-up?
- [ ] What would you change about the urgency close?
- [ ] One hypothesis to test in the next launch.

Store the debrief in your course notes or Notion tracker. Launches compound; each one should be slightly smarter than the last.
