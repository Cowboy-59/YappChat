# Worked Example: Course Launch Planner

Sample brand (for illustration only): Miriam Yates teaches a $197 self-paced course, "Calm Public Speaking," for mid-career professionals who want to present confidently at work without weeks of formal coaching. She has an email list of 1,100 subscribers and posts weekly on LinkedIn. Cart open: Monday, July 14. Cart close: Sunday, July 20 (7-day window). Founding-student bonus: a downloadable "30-Second Pre-Talk Ritual" PDF expires after 72 hours. No waitlist for this launch.

---

## Launch Window Summary

| Phase | Dates | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-launch warm-up | July 7-13 | 7 days |
| Open cart | July 14-20 | 7 days |
| Urgency close | July 19-20 (final 48 hours) | 2 days |
| Post-launch debrief | July 21-22 | 48 hours |

## Pre-Launch Warm-Up Plan (July 7-13)

**Beat 1 (July 7): The Problem Reframe**
Channel: email + LinkedIn post
Topic: "Most presentation nerves aren't about the audience. They're about the 90 seconds before you start."
Job: Reframe the problem. Public speaking fear is usually pre-talk anxiety, not mid-talk performance. This makes the course's approach (managing the before) feel immediately credible.
LinkedIn post angle: a short observation post. "If you get nervous before presenting, the talk itself is rarely the issue. It's the wait."

**Beat 2 (July 9): The Insight Share**
Channel: email only
Topic: One physical technique to lower pre-talk cortisol that takes 30 seconds.
Job: Give a genuinely useful tip for free. This builds trust and demonstrates that the paid course will be this concrete, not vague advice.
Email notes: Short, direct, no pitch. Subject: "A 30-second thing that helps with presentation nerves."

**Beat 3 (July 11): The Story**
Channel: email + LinkedIn
Topic: Miriam's own story: the presentation that made her realize advice like "just breathe" doesn't work.
Job: Create emotional connection. People trust teachers who have been in the same situation.
Email notes: 3-4 paragraphs. End with: "I figured out what actually works. I'm sharing it properly next week."

**Beat 4 (July 13): The Tease**
Channel: email only
Topic: Announcement email.
Job: Tell the list that enrollment opens Monday. Invite them to reply with one question about public speaking.
Subject: "Opening Monday: a short course on calm, confident presenting."
Content: One paragraph on who it is for, what it covers, and the founding-student bonus. CTA: "I'll be in touch Monday with the details."

---

## Open Cart Email Sequence (July 14-20)

**Email 1: Cart Open (July 14, 9am)**
Subject options: "Calm Public Speaking is open. Here's what's inside." / "Enrollment is open today only for founding students."
Job: Announce, deliver the promise, drive to the sales page.
Key points: who it is for, the three-sentence promise, what's inside (brief), founding-student bonus (72 hours), CTA. Keep to under 300 words.

**Email 2: The Story (July 15, 9am)**
Subject options: "What changed for a project manager who dreaded every all-hands." / "She went from 'my voice shakes' to presenting to 80 people."
Job: Social proof via a student story (beta student).
Key points: a specific story from a beta student. Her before state, the one thing that changed, her after. End with: "If that sounds like the shift you want, here's the link." No pitch in the body; the story is the argument.

**Email 3: The Objection Email (July 16, 9am)**
Subject options: "Is this course for you? Here's how to decide." / "What if I've tried public speaking courses before and they didn't help?"
Job: Handle the "I've tried things like this" objection.
Key points: acknowledge that most public speaking advice is generic and willpower-based. Explain the specific thing this course does differently (the pre-talk ritual, the nervous system technique, the structured rehearsal method). Offer an honest description of who it is NOT for.

**Email 4: Bonus Expiry Reminder (July 16, 5pm)**
Subject options: "The bonus PDF expires at midnight." / "Last few hours for the 30-Second Pre-Talk Ritual."
Job: Note the 72-hour founding-student bonus expiry. Short email.
Key points: name the bonus, state the expiry time, link. Under 150 words.

**Email 5: Module Spotlight (July 17, 9am)**
Subject options: "What happens in Module 2." / "The part of this course that surprises people."
Job: Add depth; give the fence-sitter one more reason to say yes.
Key points: feature Module 2 (the pre-talk ritual module) with specificity. What the student does, what they build, what they take away. One paragraph. Link.

**Email 6: FAQ (July 18, 9am)**
Subject options: "Answers to the questions you've been sending." / "A few things people have asked this week."
Job: Handle questions that came in during the launch via replies or DMs; feel personal and real.
Key points: Answer 3 real questions (examples: "How long is the course?" "Does this work for virtual presentations?" "What if I have an important presentation coming up in 2 weeks?"). Personal, conversational tone.

**Email 7: 48-Hour Warning (July 19, 9am)**
Subject options: "Calm Public Speaking closes Sunday night." / "Two days left."
Job: Urgency reminder. Real deadline: cart closes Sunday July 20 at 11:59pm.
Key points: restate the deadline, restate the promise in one sentence, CTA. Under 200 words.

---

## Urgency Close Plan (July 19-20)

**Real urgency element:** Literal cart close, Sunday July 20 at 11:59pm Pacific. No enrollment after that date until the next launch cycle.

**Final-hours email (July 20, 7pm)**
Subject: "Closing in a few hours."
Content: The course closes tonight at midnight Pacific. If you've been thinking about it, now is the time. [Course promise in one sentence.] [Link.] That's it.

No additional pressure language, no countdown timers in the email copy, no manufactured scarcity beyond the real deadline.
