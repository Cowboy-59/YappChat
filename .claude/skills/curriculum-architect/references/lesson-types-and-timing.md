# Lesson Types and Timing

How to mix lesson types across a module, pacing tables by course format, and a time-estimation guide.

---

## 1. Mixing lesson types across a module

A well-structured module is not all one type of lesson. Use the following mixing rules as a default, then adjust for the specific content.

### The standard 5-lesson module mix

| Lesson position | Recommended type | Purpose |
|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| Lesson 1 | Concept | Introduce the module's mental model or framework. |
| Lesson 2 | Demonstration or worked example | Show the concept applied in a realistic scenario. |
| Lesson 3 | Practice | Student applies the skill from Lesson 2. |
| Lesson 4 | Concept or demonstration | Introduce the module's second key idea. |
| Lesson 5 | Synthesis | Student integrates Lessons 1-4 in a complete task. |

### Variations by module purpose

**Knowledge-heavy module (lots to explain before doing):**
Lessons 1-2 concept, Lesson 3 worked example, Lesson 4 practice, Lesson 5 synthesis. The extra concept lesson is justified when the domain requires significant vocabulary before application is meaningful.

**Skill-intensive module (doing is the point):**
Lesson 1 concept (brief), Lessons 2-3 demonstration, Lessons 4-5 practice (two distinct scenarios). Synthesis is built into the second practice lesson.

**Capstone or project module:**
Lesson 1 synthesis (the main deliverable prompt), Lesson 2 review and critique guide (how to evaluate your own output), Lesson 3 refinement (applying the critique). This is shorter and centered on the student's work rather than new instruction.

## 2. Pacing tables by course format

### Self-paced video course

Ideal lesson length: 8-14 minutes. Students watch on their own schedule; attention drops sharply after 15 minutes.

| Module size | Total lessons | Ideal total video length |
|-------------|---------------|--------------------------|
| Small module | 3-4 lessons | 24-56 minutes |
| Standard module | 5 lessons | 40-70 minutes |
| Large module | 6-7 lessons | 48-98 minutes |

Target total course length by price point:

| Price range | Minimum content | Maximum content |
|-------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Under $97 | 60 minutes | 2 hours |
| $97-$297 | 2 hours | 5 hours |
| $297-$497 | 3 hours | 8 hours |
| $497-$997 | 5 hours | 12 hours |
| $997+ | 8 hours | 20+ hours (usually includes live components) |

Note: longer is not always better. A focused 3-hour course at $297 that delivers a specific result will sell and be completed at higher rates than an unfocused 10-hour course at the same price.

### Cohort or live course

Sessions are typically 60-90 minutes, including instruction and group work. Break each session into no more than two teaching segments with a group activity or discussion between them.

| Session structure | Timing |
|-------------------|--------|
| Opening and recap of previous session | 5-10 minutes |
| Teaching segment 1 | 15-20 minutes |
| Activity or discussion | 10-15 minutes |
| Teaching segment 2 (optional) | 10-15 minutes |
| Practice or group exercise | 15-20 minutes |
| Wrap-up and homework for next session | 5 minutes |

For a 6-week cohort at one session per week, each session covers approximately one module's worth of content.

### Hybrid course (self-paced content with live elements)

The video content should be shorter than a pure self-paced course (aim for 60-70% of the equivalent self-paced length). Live sessions handle application, Q&A, and accountability rather than new instruction.

## 3. Time estimation guide

Use these benchmarks when estimating lesson length during curriculum design.

### Video lessons

| Content type | Typical duration |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Conceptual introduction (framework, idea, mental model) | 6-10 minutes |
| Demonstration (screencast, process walkthrough) | 10-15 minutes |
| Worked example (talking through a complete case) | 8-12 minutes |
| Practice prompt and setup | 3-6 minutes |
| Synthesis lesson instruction | 8-12 minutes |
| Review or Q&A lesson | 10-20 minutes |

Add 20% to your first estimate for any lesson covering a dense or technical topic.

### Text-based lessons

If the course is text-first (a written course, a PDF-based format, or a reading-heavy hybrid):

- 1,000 words of instructional text takes approximately 5-7 minutes to read at a comfortable pace.
- Include practice prompts and worksheets as separate files; do not count their completion time in the lesson estimate.
- Text lessons should not exceed 1,500 words without a natural break (a subheading, a callout box, an image, or a worksheet prompt). Longer content should be split into two lessons.

### Live instruction

Spoken instruction moves more slowly than written instruction, but discussion adds depth. A concept that takes 8 minutes to explain in a video typically takes 15-20 minutes to explain live, including questions and discussion.

## 4. The quick-win checklist

The first tangible result is the most important moment in any course. Check the first 30 minutes of content against this list.

- [ ] Is there at least one practice or application lesson in the first 30 minutes of the course?
- [ ] Does that practice lesson produce something the student can see, use, or share immediately?
- [ ] Is the quick win genuinely useful on its own, not just a stepping stone to later lessons?
- [ ] Is the quick win something the student could not have produced without the course's specific approach?
- [ ] Does the lesson acknowledge the quick win explicitly? ("You just did [X]. That took most people months to figure out on their own.")

If any answer is no, restructure the early content to deliver a visible result sooner.

## 5. Module and lesson naming conventions

Names matter because they appear in the table of contents, on the sales page, and in the student's experience of making progress.

### Module names

Use outcome-oriented titles, not topic labels.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| Module 1: Introduction | Module 1: How to Think About [TOPIC] Like a Pro |
| Module 3: Pricing | Module 3: Set Prices That Reflect Your Value |
| Module 5: Marketing | Module 5: Get Your First Paying Clients Without Ads |

### Lesson names

Lesson names should describe what the student gains, not what the instructor explains.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| Lesson 2: The Fundamentals | Lesson 2: The Three Terms You Need Before Any Negotiation |
| Lesson 4: Practice | Lesson 4: Write Your First Counteroffer (Template Included) |
| Lesson 7: Review | Lesson 7: Spot the Red Flags: Review a Real Lease Clause by Clause |

A student scanning the table of contents should be able to predict exactly what they will get from each lesson. If the title could apply to five different lessons, it is too vague.
