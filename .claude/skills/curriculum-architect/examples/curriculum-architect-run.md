# Curriculum Architect: Worked Example

Sample brand (for illustration only): Diane Okafor is a corporate wellness consultant who has run workshops on stress management for over 200 teams. She is turning her core workshop into a self-paced online course for individual professionals who cannot access corporate wellness programs.

---

## Inputs

- Course topic: Sustainable stress management for busy professionals.
- Primary transformation: Professionals will go from reactive, overwhelmed, and running on willpower to operating a personalized stress-management system that reduces their baseline stress level within four weeks of starting the course.
- Target student level: Beginner to intermediate (they know stress is a problem; they do not know how to address it systematically).
- Format: Self-paced video course with downloadable worksheets.
- Target total length: 4-5 hours of video.
- Modules estimate: 5 modules.
- Terminal outcomes from audience-and-outcome-definer:
  - Identify their primary stress triggers using the Trigger Audit tool.
  - Build a personalized recovery routine using at least three evidence-based practices.
  - Design a weekly rhythm that includes proactive stress prevention, not just recovery.
  - Apply a 5-minute in-the-moment reset when stress peaks during the workday.
  - Complete a 4-week implementation plan and track their baseline stress score reduction.

---

## Output

### 1. Learning Arc Narrative

The student arrives overwhelmed, reactive, and relying on willpower to get through their week. They believe stress management means doing yoga or taking vacations, neither of which is accessible in a typical busy schedule.

The course moves them through four shifts:

First, from "stress is just life" to "stress is a system I can understand and influence" (the mental model shift). Second, from scattered coping tactics to a toolbox of specific, evidence-based practices that take under ten minutes each. Third, from knowing the tools to having a personalized routine built around their specific triggers and schedule. Fourth, from a one-time effort to a sustainable weekly rhythm they can maintain even during their busiest periods.

The student finishes with a completed stress management system: their own Trigger Audit, their personalized recovery routine, their weekly rhythm, and a 4-week implementation plan. They have also used the in-the-moment reset in a real situation at least once before the course ends.

---

### 2. Module Summaries

**Module 1: Understand Your Stress (How the System Works)**
Module outcome: Identify their top three stress triggers and understand the physiological loop that makes their current patterns feel stuck.
Completion marker: Completed Trigger Audit worksheet with ranked triggers.
Lesson count: 4 lessons.
Estimated total time: 42 minutes.

**Module 2: Your In-the-Moment Reset (The Quick Win)**
Module outcome: Use a 5-minute reset technique during or after a high-stress moment and feel a measurable difference.
Completion marker: Completed and used the 5-minute reset at least once (self-reported in the module journal prompt).
Lesson count: 4 lessons.
Estimated total time: 38 minutes.

**Module 3: Build Your Recovery Toolkit**
Module outcome: Select and practice at least three evidence-based recovery techniques suited to their lifestyle and schedule.
Completion marker: Completed personal toolkit card with three chosen techniques and a practice log for one week.
Lesson count: 5 lessons.
Estimated total time: 52 minutes.

**Module 4: Design Your Weekly Rhythm**
Module outcome: Build a weekly schedule template that includes proactive stress-prevention practices, not just recovery after the fact.
Completion marker: Completed Weekly Rhythm Template with practices assigned to specific time slots.
Lesson count: 5 lessons.
Estimated total time: 55 minutes.

**Module 5: Make It Stick**
Module outcome: Complete a 4-week implementation plan and establish a review habit that catches stress spirals before they take hold.
Completion marker: Completed 4-week plan and a written "if-then" protocol for the two most common relapse scenarios.
Lesson count: 4 lessons.
Estimated total time: 43 minutes.

Total estimated video time: approximately 3 hours 50 minutes. Worksheets and journaling add 30-60 minutes per module depending on depth of engagement. Total student time: 6-7 hours.

### 4. Sequence Audit

**Prerequisite order:** No forward references found. Module 2 (in-the-moment reset) assumes the Trigger Audit from Module 1.3 is complete, which is confirmed by the module sequence. Passed.

**Momentum curve:** The course opens with two concept lessons (1.1 and 1.2) before the first practice lesson (1.3). This is within the three-concept maximum. The difficulty is consistent across modules with practice and synthesis lessons distributed evenly. Module 4 has the highest density of applied work, which is appropriate as the construction phase. Passed.

**Practice distribution:** Every module contains at least one practice lesson and one synthesis lesson. Module 2 has four lessons that are all practice or demonstration, which is intentional given it is the Quick Win module. No module is all concept. Passed.

**Completion markers:** All five modules end with a synthesis lesson that produces a named artifact. Passed.

**Quick Win check:** Lesson 2.4 ("Use It Today") is positioned at minute 61 of the course (end of Module 2). Students who finish Module 1 and go straight into Module 2 will have their first real-world application experience by the end of their second session. This is slightly later than the 30-minute ideal; however, the Trigger Audit at 1.3 (minute 31) provides a first tangible output. The quick win is adequately positioned. Noted: frame Lesson 1.3 explicitly as "your first real tool from this course" in the lesson introduction.

**One fix applied:** Module 3 originally had six lessons. Lesson 3.2 and 3.3 were combined into a single 21-minute lesson, which exceeded the recommended maximum. Both were retained as separate lessons to keep each under 12 minutes and maintain a clear distinction between movement-based and cognitive recovery.
